**If a transfer fee appears in your Houston closing file, ask the title company for three things: the document that creates the charge, the party receiving it, and the reason it is being charged on this sale.** A fee label on a worksheet does not tell you whether it is a routine association charge or a private obligation tied to a property transfer.

## Find the source before deciding who pays

Start with the page of the title commitment or fee estimate that raised the question. Ask the closer to point to the exact document and clause, and to confirm it covers the property you are selling. A commitment lists conditions or exceptions the title company has identified; it is not a ruling on the fee. The [Texas Department of Insurance title FAQ](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/title/titlefaqs.html) explains how the commitment fits into the sale.

For a recorded covenant, ask for the instrument number and copies of any amendment, notice or release the title team is relying on. Use the county where the property sits, even if the mailing address says Houston. The [Harris County Clerk's real property office](https://www.cclerk.hctx.net/RealProperty.aspx) and the [Fort Bend County Clerk's recording office](https://www.fortbendcountytx.gov/government/departments/county-clerk/recording/real-and-personal-property) provide access to their respective records. A county record is a starting point for review; it does not decide what your contract requires.

## Ask whether the charge is a covenant fee or something else

[Texas Property Code Chapter 5, Subchapter G](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.5.htm#5.201) generally voids covered private transfer fee obligations created on or after June 17, 2011, as to subsequent owners and purchasers. It also sets notice requirements for certain older obligations. The law excludes some charges from the definition, including qualifying association charges and government recording fees. An HOA name on an invoice alone does not settle the classification, especially if the money is passed on to another party.

Ask for each charge as a separate line. The [HOA resale documents guide](/blog/hoa-resale-documents-texas-house-sale/) covers routine association paperwork; a claimed fee payable to a separate party under a covenant needs its own review. If the title team flags a possible private transfer fee, ask what remains unresolved and who will address the written notice required by Section 5.205 when applicable. If the fee's status is disputed, have a Texas real estate attorney review the documents before you agree to pay or omit it.

## Compare offers using the same verified fee assumptions

Ask for an updated seller net sheet that shows the charge, who pays it under the deal, and whether the amount is confirmed or still under review. **Hypothetical:** If your estimated seller proceeds are $82,000 before a disputed $1,500 charge, the estimate would be $80,500 if you ultimately owe it and $82,000 if it does not apply, assuming all other items stay the same. Neither figure tells you whether the fee is enforceable.

A listing can reach more buyers and may produce a higher price. A direct cash offer may reduce prep and financing steps but can come in lower, and it does not clear a title issue by itself. Use the [same seller net worksheet](/compare-the-net/) for both routes. If you [ask Cash As-Is for a written offer](https://www.cashasis.com/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=cta&utm_campaign=blog_cta&utm_content=private-transfer-fee-selling-houston-house&utm_term=inline), mention the disputed line early and ask how the offer treats it while your title team checks the documents.
