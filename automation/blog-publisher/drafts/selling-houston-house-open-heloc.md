**You can usually sell a Houston house with an open home equity line of credit, but identify it before you rely on a closing date or a take-home amount. Ask the closing team to coordinate the payoff, account closure and required lien documentation with the lender. A low or zero balance is not the whole answer.**

This guide concerns an existing revolving credit line secured by the house. It is not a recommendation to borrow more money, a lender's payoff instruction or a legal opinion about your property. For a Harris or Fort Bend County owner, the useful starting point is the same: give the professionals reviewing the sale a complete account list.

## Know which number you are looking at

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that a HELOC is an open-end credit line](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-home-equity-line-of-credit-heloc-en-107/) that permits repeated borrowing against home equity. The approved credit limit, amount currently borrowed and amount required to pay off the account answer different questions.

An online balance can help you identify the account, but do not turn that screen into your final sale-proceeds calculation. Ask which payoff document the closing office needs, who will obtain it and what date it covers. Include the HELOC even if another company handles your first mortgage.

## Separate paying down the balance from closing the line

[Chase's HELOC servicing instructions](https://www.chase.com/personal/home-equity/customer-service/info/pay-off-account) provide a concrete example: an account with a zero balance can remain open during its draw period, and its lien remains while the line is open. Chase describes requesting a payoff quote and giving authorization to close. Those are that lender's instructions, not a universal form or deadline.

For your account, ask the actual lender to confirm its process. Keep three questions separate: what must be paid, what ends access to the credit line and what documentation addresses the lien for this sale. Do not assume that sending a payment answers all three.

If your issue is an old mortgage that was already paid and its release cannot be matched, our [paid-off mortgage release guide](/blog/paid-off-mortgage-lien-release-houston/) covers that different records problem. Here, the additional concern is a revolving line that may still be available to use.

## Keep the payoff information current

[Chase's home-sale explanation](https://www.chase.com/personal/mortgage/education/financing-a-home/what-happens-to-heloc-when-you-sell-your-house) says a HELOC generally needs to be paid off when the secured house is sold. It also notes that account terms, fees, further withdrawals and administrative steps can affect the closing process.

Bring the lender's requirements into the planning conversation early. If account activity or the expected closing date changes, tell the closing team instead of continuing to rely on an older estimate. Ask how any pending activity should be handled; do not treat unused credit as guaranteed moving money.

Use a verified secure channel for statements and authorizations. Do not send account details through an unexpected text or copy payment instructions from a general article. The closing professionals should confirm the transaction's actual instructions with the appropriate parties.

## Estimate proceeds without counting the credit limit as debt

Consider a **hypothetical arithmetic example, not an offer or closing estimate**. A seller compares a $200,000 sale price with a $140,000 first-mortgage payoff and a $20,000 HELOC payoff. Subtracting those two assumed payoffs leaves $40,000 **before all other selling costs, taxes, credits and adjustments**.

An approved $50,000 credit limit is not an additional $50,000 of borrowed principal in that example. Likewise, the $40,000 subtotal is not a promise of money available to move. The closing team's complete figures determine the transaction estimate. Our [seller net-proceeds comparison](/compare-the-net/) can help organize assumptions; it does not replace payoff quotes or the settlement statement.

If the proposed sale proceeds appear insufficient, raise that with the closing team and lender before making commitments. Do not assume a buyer can forgive the balance or promise a lender-approved solution yourself.

## Compare the purchase offer separately

A listing can provide broader exposure when you can manage preparation and showings. A current-condition Cash As-Is evaluation may fit an owner who wants to discuss the house without renovating first. Either route still needs the credit line handled correctly.

Compare the written price, responsibilities and expected net rather than assuming a cash offer automatically leaves more money. For wider title questions, see the [Houston lien and title overview](/blog/sell-house-with-liens-houston/).

When asking Cash As-Is to evaluate the property, mention that a HELOC is open and whether payoff review has started. We can discuss the condition and purchase fit while the appropriate professionals work through the account requirements. That keeps an avoidable paperwork surprise from becoming the main conversation at the end.
