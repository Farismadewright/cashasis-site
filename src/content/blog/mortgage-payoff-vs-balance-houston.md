**Your mortgage statement balance may not be the amount required to pay off the loan when you sell.** Before deciding what a Houston cash offer leaves in your pocket, obtain a dated payoff quote and compare it with the closing agent's itemized estimate.

This matters whether the house is in Harris County or Fort Bend County, needs repairs, or is ready for the market. An attractive purchase price is useful. A clear picture of what remains after closing is more useful.

## Start with three separate numbers

Keep these figures on separate lines:

1. **Purchase price:** the amount the buyer agrees to pay under the contract.
2. **Mortgage payoff:** the amount the servicer quotes to settle the loan for a specified date.
3. **Estimated seller proceeds:** the amount remaining after the payoff and other applicable settlement items.

You can use a statement balance for an early rough comparison, but label it provisional. Avoid committing the same expected dollars to a move, another purchase, or overdue bills before the final figures are reconciled.

For a broader comparison of sale expenses, use our [seller net comparison guide](/compare-the-net/). This article focuses on the mortgage number inside that calculation.

## Why a payoff quote can be different

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains the distinction](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-payoff-amount-and-is-it-the-same-as-my-current-balance-en-205/): a payoff can include interest through the intended payoff date and unpaid charges. A prepayment penalty can apply to some loans; do not assume your loan has one.

Request the quote from your servicer for the expected payment date. Ask it to explain any item you cannot reconcile with your statement. The goal is to understand the actual loan figures, rather than guess at a standard fee or percentage.

### Ask what happens if the date moves

If closing or delivery of payoff funds changes, ask the closing agent to confirm the quote's coverage. Put the expected payment date next to the quote in your notes. A document can be accurate for its stated date and still need attention when the transaction moves.

The difference is **$1,250**. Nothing about the purchase price changed; the mortgage estimate changed. Your own result could be different in amount or direction. Replace every assumption with your contract, servicer quote, and closing estimate.

Do not turn this example into a reason to choose cash automatically. A listing may produce a better outcome for a seller who can accommodate its preparation, marketing, and closing process. A direct as-is purchase may fit someone who values less preparation and a simpler buyer process. Compare both using consistent obligations and realistic timelines.

## Check the rest of the closing estimate

Ask for the itemized paperwork early enough to review it calmly. The [Texas Department of Insurance's title FAQ](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/title/titlefaqs.html) notes that closing charges can vary and that buyers and sellers can negotiate who pays the title premium. It also recommends reviewing additional charges and requesting documentation.

For your estimate, ask the closing agent to separate:

- the mortgage payoff from other loan or lien items;
- property-related obligations and adjustments from transaction fees;
- expenses assigned to you from expenses assigned to the buyer; and
- confirmed figures from estimates awaiting documents.

CashAsIs does not charge a seller a real estate commission for its direct purchase. The purchase agreement identifies the closing expenses we agree to cover. Your existing mortgage and other property obligations still need to be accounted for; a buyer's payment of agreed closing expenses does not make them disappear.

If liens are involved, read our [Houston property lien guide](/blog/sell-house-with-liens-houston/) and ask the closing professionals what documents they need. Questions about a disputed debt or your legal rights belong with the appropriate professional.

## Keep mortgage escrow separate until it is confirmed

An escrow balance should not become an automatic extra credit in your estimate. Under [the CFPB's current escrow rule](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1024/34/), remaining funds generally must be returned after a covered loan is paid in full, subject to the rule's conditions. Its interpretation also permits applying remaining escrow against the outstanding loan balance.

Ask the servicer how it will handle your account. Then ask the closing agent where any adjustment appears. If money is already reflected in the payoff, counting it again as an expected refund would overstate what you receive. Keep a possible later refund separate from money confirmed for closing.

## A short checklist before accepting an offer

Use these questions for a direct buyer or a traditional sale:

1. Is this an actual written offer or only a possible asking price?
2. Is the mortgage figure a current payoff quote for the expected date?
3. Has the closing agent reviewed other obligations affecting the property?
4. Does the estimate follow the contract's allocation of expenses?
5. Are any escrow credits or refunds counted only once?
6. What needs updating if the date or contract terms change?

Save the answers with your documents. You do not need to know every closing detail before asking about a sale, but you should know which figures remain provisional before relying on them.
