**If mold work was performed on a Texas house, find the actual remediation documents before selling—not just the contractor's invoice.** For a Houston-area seller, the key questions are what was documented, whether the underlying moisture problem was addressed, and which records must go to the buyer.

A completed project and a well-organized sale file are related, but they are not the same task. Neither fresh paint nor an as-is offer should be used as a substitute for understanding the paperwork.

## Locate the Certificate of Mold Damage Remediation

The [Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation's Consumer Mold Information Sheet](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mld/pdf/cmis.pdf) explains the Certificate of Mold Damage Remediation, often shortened to CMDR. It describes documentation of the contamination identified for the project and whether the underlying cause was corrected.

TDLR also states that, when selling the property, the owner must provide the buyer copies of the certificates received for that property during the preceding five years. This is a specific record handoff, separate from the wider condition-disclosure conversation.

Use our [Texas seller-disclosure checklist](/blog/texas-as-is-sale-seller-disclosure-checklist/) alongside the certificate review. Have your agent or real estate attorney address the requirements for the actual transaction rather than treating one document as a replacement for another.

## Read the project scope before describing the whole house

Ask which rooms or areas the project covered, what the assessment identified and which documents explain the completed work. Keep the certificate with the associated reports instead of sharing it without context.

A document about a defined project should not be expanded into a promise that the entire house will never develop moisture or mold problems again. Similarly, an invoice showing that a leak was repaired does not automatically establish every other finding someone might expect from a remediation project.

Avoid reducing the history to either all fixed or a mold house. A dated, specific account is more useful to someone evaluating the property.

## Missing paperwork needs clarification, not a recreated conclusion

The [TDLR mold FAQs](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mld/mldfaq.htm) explain distinctions involving protocols, assessments, certificates and work performed under exemptions. The process used matters. Do not assume any company that cleaned an area can simply issue the certificate afterward.

Start by asking the original professionals for copies of what they issued. If a certificate was not issued, ask a properly qualified professional to explain which documents exist and whether further assessment is appropriate. Do not fill in a certificate yourself, describe a general receipt as clearance, or invent an inspection result to complete a sale file.

If current moisture or a musty condition is unresolved, describe that separately from older work. This article is about sale preparation, not mold identification, health-risk assessment or do-it-yourself remediation.

## Keep an open insurance claim on its own checklist

A remediation record and an insurance payment answer different questions. If a claim remains open, our [insurance-claim sale guide](/blog/selling-houston-house-open-insurance-claim/) helps separate claim status and proceeds from the purchase.

Likewise, compare any remaining building work with the broader [Houston major-repair selling option](/blog/sell-house-needs-major-repairs-houston/). Do not order cosmetic preparation before understanding what the condition and documents actually establish.

## Compare a listing plan with an informed as-is offer

A listing may reach more buyers and support a higher price when the condition and preparation fit that route. A Cash As-Is proposal may reduce the renovation coordination you undertake while reflecting the property's current condition and uncertainty.

Give each buyer or professional a consistent account of known history. Tell Cash As-Is what work was performed, which records you have and what is still being checked when requesting an offer review. You do not need to claim the house has no problems to begin, and a direct purchase does not erase applicable information duties.
