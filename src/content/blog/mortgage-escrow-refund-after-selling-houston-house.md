**After selling a Houston house and paying off its mortgage, ask the servicer how the remaining mortgage escrow balance will be handled.** It may be refunded separately or already accounted for in the payoff. Do not add an expected refund to your moving budget until you know which treatment applies.

This is about the mortgage account used for charges such as property taxes and insurance. It is not a buyer deposit, a Cash As-Is earnest-money requirement or a promise that every seller receives a refund. Your actual payoff and account reconciliation control the numbers.

## Keep three numbers separate

The purchase price is the amount agreed for the property. Closing proceeds are what the settlement calculation shows after the transaction's deductions and credits. A mortgage escrow balance is a separate account item that needs reconciliation with the servicer.

A large escrow figure on a recent statement does not establish how much will reach you after closing. Ask whether it remains available, has been applied within the payoff, or needs a final account adjustment. This is a request for the actual accounting, not a reason to stop making required payments before the servicer confirms payoff.

Our [property-tax proration guide](/blog/property-tax-proration-selling-houston-house/) covers the different question of tax allocation in the sale. A tax proration and a mortgage escrow refund should not be treated as interchangeable simply because both involve taxes.

## What the federal escrow-refund rule says

For covered mortgage servicing, [Regulation X, section 1024.34](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1024/34/) generally requires remaining escrow funds under the servicer's control to be returned within 20 days of full mortgage payment, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal public holidays. The official interpretation permits netting remaining escrow against the mortgage balance. The rule also allows an agreed transfer to a qualifying new mortgage escrow account under its stated conditions.

That is a rule about handling remaining funds, not a guarantee that a check arrives 20 calendar days after you sign sale documents. Ask the servicer to identify the payoff date, account treatment and any applicable exception before calculating a follow-up date for your situation.

## A two-column check prevents double counting

The following is a **hypothetical accounting comparison**. It assumes the only difference between two examples is how the same $1,800 escrow balance is handled. These are not quotes or predictions about your servicer.

| Example treatment | Closing cash | Separate later refund | Combined amount if confirmed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Escrow returned separately | $42,000 | $1,800 | $43,800 |
| Same escrow credited within payoff | $43,800 | $0 | $43,800 |

</div>

Counting $43,800 at closing **and** another $1,800 refund in the second example would overstate the total by $1,800. The labels matter as much as the arithmetic.

The timing also matters. In the first example, an anticipated later refund is not cash available for a same-day moving invoice. Make near-term plans from verified available funds, then update them when a separate refund is actually received. Actual tax or insurance disbursements and account adjustments can change the amount; this example intentionally holds those variables constant.

## Make a short post-payoff follow-up list

Before closing, identify the servicer's trusted contact route and ask how you will receive the final account information. Keep your mailing address current through the servicer's verified process if you are moving.

After payoff, retain the confirmation, the settlement statement and any refund or credit record together. Ask the servicer to explain a difference rather than assuming a missing check means the funds vanished. If its explanation still does not reconcile, ask what formal information-request or complaint process is appropriate; get professional guidance for a disputed account.

Do not confuse an escrow reconciliation with proof that a mortgage release has been recorded. The separate [paid-off mortgage release checklist](/blog/paid-off-mortgage-lien-release-houston/) helps you organize that title question.

## Evaluate offers using the same accounting assumptions

When comparing buyers, use an equivalent treatment of the mortgage payoff, seller deductions and any escrow credit. Otherwise an accounting difference can make one proposal look larger without changing the overall dollars.

A listed sale may offer wider market exposure and a different preparation burden. A Cash As-Is proposal evaluates the house in its current condition and may simplify renovation and showing demands. Neither sale route independently determines how your mortgage servicer handles escrow.
