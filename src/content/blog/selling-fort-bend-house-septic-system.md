**Before selling a Fort Bend County house with a septic system, gather its permit, operating and maintenance records—and confirm what must happen when ownership changes.** If the system has problems, get a qualified assessment before treating a quick service visit as a complete repair plan.

The seller's decision has two parts: what condition the buyer is taking on, and what records or responsibilities must transfer. A cash offer can be one option, but it does not turn an uncertain system into a documented one.

This guide concerns preparation for a property sale, not instructions for repairing wastewater equipment or a determination that a specific system complies with local requirements.

## Separate a sale evaluation from permit requirements

The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality's [septic-system owner guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfadvice.html) says a mortgage company may require an evaluation before a sale. TCEQ does not require or regulate that type of evaluation. It also advises a new owner to notify the local permitting authority and ask about proper startup procedures.

That is not a blanket exemption from local rules or the purchase agreement. Ask the responsible authority which requirements apply to the address, and ask the people handling the transaction what evaluation the buyer needs. Confirm jurisdiction rather than assuming every Fort Bend mailing address has identical procedures.

## Make a small septic handoff folder

An on-site sewage facility is often abbreviated OSSF in official records. Use that term when asking for the property's file, but do not worry if your own paperwork simply calls it a septic system.

<div style="overflow-x:auto" role="region" aria-label="Septic system seller handoff documents" tabindex="0">

| Record | What to clarify before closing |
|---|---|
| Available construction and operating documents | What was authorized, and what remains unresolved? |
| Planning materials or system layout | Where are the system components shown? |
| Service and maintenance reports | What work was done and what was recommended? |
| Current maintenance agreement, if applicable | Who handles service during the ownership change? |
| Notices or known operating problems | What needs professional follow-up? |
| Instructions from the responsible authority | How will ownership and any required documents be updated? |

</div>

If something is missing, label it missing. A recent invoice may help explain a service visit, but it is not a substitute for every other document in the folder. Record what you know without promising a trouble-free system.

## Fort Bend's local guidance changes the repair conversation

[Fort Bend Environmental Health's OSSF page](https://www.fortbendcountytx.gov/government/departments/health-and-human-services/environmental-health/on-site-sewage-facility-permits-packets) says existing systems are not grandfathered against its modification requirements. It specifically includes moving sprinklers or adding lines in work requiring a new permit and approval process. It also distinguishes an approved application from approval to install or operate the system.

Before authorizing changes to make the yard easier to market, ask how those changes affect the system and required approvals. Do not let a cosmetic selling plan become unreviewed septic work.

### Check whether a continuous-service affidavit applies

The County links a [maintenance affidavit for systems requiring continuous service](https://fbctxdocs.fortbendcountytx.gov/Forms_Library/On-Site%20Sewage%20Facility%20Packet/OnSiteSewageFacilityMainte%20%281%29.pdf). That form addresses permit transfer and submission of a signed service policy within 30 days after transfer. It is a system-specific document, not proof that every septic system has identical obligations.

Ask Environmental Health and the service provider what applies to your property and how the handoff should be documented. Confirm the current process rather than relying only on an old copy in your drawer.

## If there are problems, compare two written paths

Get a professional diagnosis, scope and estimate. Do not infer that pumping, replacing one component or changing the landscaping will resolve an unexplained problem. Avoid guessing at repair costs based on a neighbor's different system.

One path is to complete appropriate work and market the property with clearer condition information. Another is to seek an as-is proposal that accounts for the known condition and remaining uncertainty. A broader listing may produce a better price, while an as-is sale may reduce the work you personally manage. Compare the likely net result and responsibilities, not just the headline offers.
