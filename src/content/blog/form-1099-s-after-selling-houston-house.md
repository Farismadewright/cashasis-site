**If you receive Form 1099-S after selling your Houston house, keep it with the final closing statement and take both to your tax preparer.** A proceeds figure on the form is not automatically the money wired to you, and neither figure alone determines your taxable gain.

For a seller in Harris County or Fort Bend County, this distinction can prevent a stressful misunderstanding: a large number on a tax document does not mean that entire number was profit you kept. This guide helps organize records and questions. It does not calculate your tax bill or tell you which return to file.

## Put three numbers in different columns

The IRS describes [Form 1099-S](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1099-s) as a form for reporting a real-estate sale or exchange. Start by identifying the transaction and tax year shown, then separate:

- **Reported proceeds:** The figure reported for the transaction under the applicable form instructions.
- **Closing cash:** What remained for disbursement after the payoff amounts and other items actually handled in your sale.
- **Tax gain or loss:** A separate calculation requiring the property's tax history.

The IRS's [Publication 523, Selling Your Home](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p523), explains the relationship between selling price, selling expenses, amount realized and adjusted basis. Its currently available 2025 edition is a reference for those distinctions, not a substitute for instructions applicable to your sale year. Do not use this article as a checklist for a particular year's form boxes or exclusion eligibility.

## A simple closing-cash example

**Hypothetical example only:** Suppose one seller receives a $300,000 all-cash purchase price, with an assumed $180,000 mortgage payoff and $10,000 in other seller deductions. The arithmetic leaves **$110,000** before any items omitted from those assumptions.

That illustrates a closing-cash calculation: $300,000 minus $180,000 minus $10,000. It is not a tax-gain calculation, a prediction of your proceeds or an instruction for completing Form 1099-S. The example does not supply an adjusted basis or decide which expenses receive a particular tax treatment.

For the actual transaction, the [net-proceeds comparison](/compare-the-net/) can help organize sale assumptions. Your final statement and professional review remain necessary.

## Build one sale-record folder

Gather documents that let the reviewer trace what happened rather than relying on a bank deposit screenshot:

1. The complete Form 1099-S or substitute statement received, including issuer information.
2. The final settlement statement and any corrected version.
3. The sale agreement and amendments affecting price or responsibilities.
4. Your acquisition records and available improvement records.
5. Information about rental, business, inherited or other use relevant to your tax preparer's questions.

Do not decide from that list that every receipt is deductible or that every payment increases basis. Mark missing documents and ask which ones matter. A mortgage payoff record explains a disbursement; it does not, by itself, establish the property's tax basis.

Our [mortgage escrow refund guide](/blog/mortgage-escrow-refund-after-selling-houston-house/) addresses another amount that may arrive separately from closing cash. Keeping the refund, sale proceeds and tax calculation distinct avoids counting the same money twice or assigning it the wrong meaning.

## If the figures appear inconsistent

Write down the specific mismatch: the transaction, date, ownership allocation or amount you cannot reconcile. Ask the issuer to explain the source of the reported information and whether a correction is needed. Give your tax preparer both the original and any corrected document.

Protect the private information involved. Use a trusted channel and independently verify the closing office's contact details before sending identity or tax documents. Do not attach an unredacted form to a public social post seeking an answer. Our [closing-payment fraud guide](/blog/protect-house-sale-proceeds-wire-fraud/) covers related verification habits.

An online example cannot resolve a multi-owner allocation, inherited basis, rental history or an unusual payment arrangement. Those details belong in your professional review, not a guess based on the sale price alone.

## Keep the sale decision separate from the tax decision

A listing may offer broader market exposure and a higher price; an as-is cash proposal may reduce preparation and showing demands but can have a lower price. Neither route establishes your tax outcome. Compare written terms, actual seller costs and the work each option requires without treating a tax form as proof that one buyer is better.

When discussing a Cash As-Is offer, start with the property's condition, ownership and desired timing. For tax questions, keep the complete sale file and ask a qualified preparer about your circumstances and the correct year's guidance. Clear records are more useful than assuming the biggest number, or the closing wire, is your profit.
