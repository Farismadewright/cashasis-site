**If you are considering selling a Houston house during mortgage forbearance, start with the servicer's written arrangement and a current sale-payoff review.** A payment pause does not mean the debt has disappeared, and the normal monthly statement is not a complete sale plan.

You also do not have to assume selling is the only choice. Ask about available repayment help while comparing what a sale would leave you after the actual obligations and selling costs. The right decision depends on your household, loan terms and property—not simply the fact that payments were paused.

## Understand what the pause does

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains forbearance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-mortgage-forbearance-en-289/) as a temporary pause or reduction in mortgage payments. The missed amounts still must be repaid. It advises asking about the payment amount, duration, interest and repayment terms.

Keep the approved arrangement and any later changes together. A phone conversation you remember differently from the servicer is a reason to obtain clarification, not a basis for estimating what the closing will require.

Do not apply an old pandemic program description or someone else's repayment result to your loan. Ask the servicer which options apply to your account now.

## Separate keeping the house from paying off the loan

The CFPB's [guide to exiting forbearance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/housing/housing-insecurity/help-for-homeowners/exit-your-forbearance-carefully/) recommends contacting the servicer before the arrangement ends. It describes possible repayment approaches, including plans, deferrals, modifications and lump-sum repayment, with availability depending on the loan and circumstances.

Ask two separate questions: “What could let me keep the house?” and “What must be resolved if I sell?” A repayment arrangement is not automatically the same as a sale payoff. A HUD-approved housing counselor can help you understand the options without assuming a buyer's proposal is your only path.

If an FHA partial claim is involved, our [separate partial-claim payoff guide](/blog/fha-partial-claim-selling-houston-house/) explains why that obligation needs its own attention. Do not assume every forbearance produces that type of debt.

## Use a sale-readiness worksheet

<div style="overflow-x:auto" role="region" aria-label="Forbearance sale preparation worksheet" tabindex="0">

| Information to obtain | Question it should answer |
|---|---|
| Current forbearance arrangement | What payment change was approved, and when does it end? |
| Repayment or follow-up notices | What happens next under this account's terms? |
| Current payoff review | What amount and conditions apply to the proposed closing? |
| Any separate assistance obligation | Does another payoff or release need coordination? |
| Written selling-cost estimate | What could remain after the verified obligations and costs? |

</div>

Ask the closing team and servicer to resolve differences. Do not add missed payments to a payoff a second time if they are already included. Equally, do not subtract an expected credit that has not been confirmed. This worksheet organizes questions; it is not a determination of the amount owed.

## Keep notices and deadlines visible

Tell the servicer and closing professional about any notice you have received and any proposed sale date. Request a named next step and written confirmation of what remains unresolved. A purchase agreement alone does not prove the mortgage issue is settled.

If foreclosure is a concern, use our existing [Houston foreclosure-options guide](/blog/stop-foreclosure-houston/) as a starting point for questions and obtain prompt qualified help. This article does not promise that forbearance stops every foreclosure action, that a sale will close in time or that a buyer can change a lender's requirements.

## Compare realistic paths, including staying

A repayment option may suit an owner who wants to remain and can sustain the resulting obligations. Listing may suit a seller who wants broad exposure and can manage the preparation and marketing period. A Cash As-Is evaluation may suit a seller who prefers a current-condition discussion and fewer preparation tasks.

Compare the actual purchase price, costs, access requirements and confirmed closing conditions. A cash offer may trade price for convenience; it is not financially superior in every case. For Houston-area sellers, the useful first conversation includes the property's condition, the desired timing and the fact that servicer review is still underway. Keep account documents private and share them only with the professionals who need them through verified channels.
