**If a buyer’s lender appraisal comes in below the agreed price on your Houston house, ask what actually changed in the financing before deciding whether to reduce the price.** Check the signed agreement and its deadlines at the same time. A lower opinion of value, a smaller loan and a buyer’s right to end the deal are separate questions.

For a seller in Harris or Fort Bend County, the immediate task is to get the appraisal result, the buyer’s proposed response and the applicable paperwork into one conversation. Don’t negotiate from a secondhand message that only says the appraisal came in low.

## Get three numbers before you discuss a discount

Start with the price in the signed agreement. Then confirm the appraiser’s opinion of value and ask the buyer, through the people handling the transaction, what loan amount the lender is now prepared to use. Keep any unconfirmed figure clearly marked.

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau’s low-appraisal guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/my-appraisal-is-less-than-the-sale-price-what-does-that-mean-for-me-en-2007/) explains why a lower value can prompt a price negotiation. It describes an appraisal as an opinion of value and notes that the contract matters if a buyer wants to cancel. That is different from saying a report sets a new sale price.

**Hypothetical example, not a Houston valuation or a lending rule:** A house is under contract for $250,000 and the appraisal says $240,000. The value gap is $10,000. Suppose the lender separately confirms that the proposed loan drops from $200,000 to $192,000. At the same price, the buyer’s price contribution rises from $50,000 to $58,000 before closing costs. The extra contribution in this example is $8,000, not the $10,000 value gap. Get the real loan figures before discussing who can cover what.

## Check which document governs the next step

Gather the signed contract, financing addendum, any appraisal addendum and any written notices already received. Ask the appropriate agent or Texas real estate attorney to identify the relevant provision and the next deadline. Ask which documents or notices matter and whether anything has been agreed in writing. This guide does not determine either party’s rights.

The [Texas Real Estate Commission’s forms directory](https://www.trec.texas.gov/node/34) lists Form 49-1, its [lender appraisal addendum](https://www.trec.texas.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-forms/49-1.pdf). That form has different choices and is limited to certain transactions with the Third Party Financing Addendum; its instructions exclude FHA and VA financing. Do not assume it is attached, that a particular choice was selected, or that it governs a Cash As-Is purchase. Use the documents signed for your sale.

Keep the appraisal separate from a home inspection. The [CFPB’s inspection guide](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/close/schedule-a-home-inspection/) distinguishes a condition assessment from an appraisal. If the number being discussed came from a county tax notice instead, see our [Harris County tax-value comparison](/blog/hcad-tax-value-vs-cash-offer-harris-county/) for that different question.

## Compare the paths you can actually take

If the buyer can still close on the agreed terms, ask what remains to confirm. If the buyer proposes a change, ask for the revised price or financing plan and a realistic date. Compare what you would receive after payoffs, concessions and selling costs. A price reduction only makes sense if the updated deal works for you.

If the current agreement is properly resolved and you are free to consider other buyers, relisting can give you broader exposure and a chance at a higher price. It also adds time and leaves open the possibility of another financing issue. A direct cash proposal can remove the retail buyer’s mortgage approval step, but it may be lower and still depends on the written terms and closing work.
