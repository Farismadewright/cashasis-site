**Before offering a manufactured house and land for sale near Houston, have the land ownership and the manufactured-unit ownership records reviewed together.** Do not assume a land deed, a property-tax account or the fact that the house has been there for years answers every transfer question.

For a Harris or Fort Bend County seller, the practical goal is a clear description of what you own, what the proposal includes and what must be resolved before closing. This is a preparation guide, not a determination of ownership, licensing eligibility or legal transfer requirements for a particular property.

## Start with two record questions

The [Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs ownership FAQ](https://www.tdhca.texas.gov/frequently-asked-questions-statement-ownership) explains that its Statement of Ownership confirms ownership and liens recorded with the department. The land deed and that manufactured-unit record serve different purposes.

Put the available documents in separate folders. One concerns the parcel. The other concerns the manufactured house and its identifying information. Then ask the closing professional to match them. Similar names or a shared mailing address are not a substitute for that review.

If you are gathering the land-side paperwork, our [existing-survey guide](/blog/existing-survey-when-selling-texas-house/) explains a different preparation step. A survey drawing does not replace the unit's ownership record.

## Check the election, not just the foundation

TDHCA says manufactured houses default to personal property unless the required real-property election process is completed. Its guidance discusses the application, documentation and county recording involved in conversion. Do not conclude that a porch, skirting or permanent-looking installation proves the process is complete.

Ask the title professional or Texas real-estate attorney to confirm how the unit is currently recorded and which records are needed for the intended sale. Do not start a conversion simply because a buyer describes it as easy. First understand whether it is needed, who handles it and how it affects the proposed transaction.

This article does not provide conversion instructions, calculate fees or promise an approval date.

## Make the proposed sale scope explicit

Before comparing prices, ask what each proposal actually includes:

- The land and the manufactured house remaining in place?
- The land with a separate unresolved unit-ownership question?
- A unit-only transaction involving different arrangements?
- Other improvements, belongings or equipment that need their own agreement?

These are questions to clarify, not assumptions about what Cash As-Is purchases. A price for a unit that must be moved is not directly comparable with a price for a parcel and dwelling together. Ask who would be responsible for each task and what is still unconfirmed.

For example, imagine a hypothetical owner who finds an old land-closing folder but no current unit record. The useful next action is requesting the appropriate record review. Repainting the house would not answer the ownership question. Nor would describing the entire package as “clear title” without confirmation.

## Handle missing or conflicting information early

TDHCA's FAQ addresses missing records and corrections. It explains that supporting ownership evidence may be needed and that the department reviews what is submitted. Keep the actual documents and questions together rather than treating an old bill of sale as automatic clearance.

Use a short review log: document supplied, discrepancy identified, person responsible and next confirmed step. Share identification and financial paperwork only through verified private channels. Do not publish serial numbers or sensitive documents in an advertisement to prove ownership.

If the land or unit involves multiple owners, an estate or another authority issue, tell the professionals at the start. Our [Houston title-problem guide](/blog/sell-house-title-problems-houston/) provides broader seller context, but neither article replaces a property-specific review.

## Compare selling choices after defining the package

A properly supported listing may provide wider exposure when you can accommodate the preparation and marketing process. A current-condition purchase proposal may appeal when you want to discuss the property's existing condition and a practical handoff. Compare actual price, obligations, costs and confirmed timing for the same package.

Ask whether your specific property fits Cash As-Is before making plans around an offer. This guide does not promise that we buy every manufactured unit, act as a manufactured-housing retailer or arrange transport. Explain the location, condition and whether land is included; flag any ownership review still pending.

For a property that fits, an evaluation can help you compare a direct sale with other options. A cash proposal does not settle ownership discrepancies or make the paperwork optional.
