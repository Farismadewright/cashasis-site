**A tenant's security deposit belongs in the sale handoff, not in a forgotten folder after closing.** Before selling an occupied Houston-area rental, reconcile the deposit records and ask the buyer and closing professionals how the funds, responsibility and tenant paperwork will be handled.

This is a document-preparation guide for an ordinary voluntary sale of a Texas residential rental, including properties in Harris County and Fort Bend County. It is not a lease interpretation or a determination of anyone's liability.

## Start with the Texas rule

[Texas Property Code §92.105](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/pr/htm/pr.92.htm#92.105) generally makes the new owner responsible for returning security deposits from acquisition of title. It requires a signed statement to the tenant acknowledging new ownership, responsibility and the exact deposit amount.

The prior owner can remain liable for a deposit received during ownership until the new owner receives it or assumes that liability, unless the parties' written contract specifies otherwise. The section has a foreclosure-lienholder exception. Section 92.106 also requires accurate deposit records.

Have a qualified Texas real estate attorney review how the actual contract and tenancy fit these rules. Do not read a sales price, a buyer's verbal assurance or the word “cash” as a complete answer.

## Build one reconciled deposit file

Start with the paperwork you already have. The most useful file makes it possible to trace the amount shown in the lease to the money received and the amount everyone proposes to recognize at closing.

Include:

- the signed lease and any amendments affecting the deposit;
- receipts, payment records and the current deposit ledger;
- a property manager's corresponding statement, if a manager holds funds;
- any written explanation of a difference between those records; and
- the proposed handoff instructions for review by the transaction professionals.

Do not fill a missing figure from memory merely to make the worksheet look finished. Mark it “not reconciled” and identify the document or person needed to resolve it.

Keep deposit information distinct from rent, prepaid rent and other charges in your working file. This organizational separation helps reviewers see the question you are asking; it does not decide the legal treatment of a disputed payment.

## Review three parts of the handoff separately

Use three headings when discussing the proposed sale:

### 1. The money or settlement adjustment

Ask what the buyer, seller and closing agent propose to do with the deposit amount. Request an explanation of any funds transfer or credit and where it appears in the closing paperwork. Check that the same amount is not included twice in your working estimate.

### 2. The written agreement

Ask your attorney how the purchase agreement addresses the deposit handoff and any unresolved issue. If you do not understand a provision, resolve that question before relying on an assumption about what happens after closing.

### 3. The tenant acknowledgment

Ask who will prepare, sign and deliver the acknowledgment discussed in the statute. Identify how its delivery will be documented. Keep the legal review, bookkeeping and communication plan connected so there is one consistent deposit amount under discussion.

These questions are useful even when everyone is cooperative. A clear file makes the proposed transaction easier to evaluate and reduces avoidable back-and-forth over missing documents.

## Be truthful about the tenancy from the start

When requesting an offer, tell the buyer that the property is occupied. Explain what records are available, whether a property manager is involved and whether there are unresolved questions. Share necessary documents through appropriate private channels; do not put tenant details in a public listing or marketing post.

## Compare sale routes without skipping the paperwork

An occupied rental might fit a direct investor purchase or a listing strategy aimed at suitable buyers. Compare real proposals, property condition, access requirements and the work each route would require. Do not assume a cash offer will be financially superior or that it makes a tenancy problem disappear.
