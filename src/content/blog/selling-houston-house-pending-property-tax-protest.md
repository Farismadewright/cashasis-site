**If you are selling a Houston house while a property tax protest is still open, give the closing team the protest status and hearing notice before it prepares your seller net sheet. Ask who will handle the case after ownership changes and how the signed deal treats a later bill change.** Do not budget for proposed tax savings as if they have already arrived.

## Check whether the protest is still pending

Put the property account, tax year, filing confirmation, and latest hearing notice or decision in one packet. Keep account details in the closing team's secure channel. Verify the appraisal district from the property's records before assuming the mailing city identifies it. Harris County sellers can check the [HCAD protest process](https://hcad.org/hcad-help/protests-and-corrections/ifile-and-isettle); Fort Bend sellers should use the appraisal district shown on the account.

A filed protest, an offered settlement and a final decision are different stages. The [Texas Comptroller's protest guide](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/protests/) explains that the appraisal review board issues a written order after ruling. If someone tells you the value was reduced, ask for the document and whether the tax collector has posted a new bill. A lower appraisal value by itself is not a confirmed closing credit or a cash refund.

## Confirm who takes over if closing comes first

Under [Texas Tax Code Section 41.412(b)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TX/htm/TX.41.htm#41.412), when ownership changes during a pending protest, the new owner may apply to the appraisal review board to proceed with it. Ask the board what it needs from the new owner and how it will handle a hearing already on the calendar. Do not assume the buyer has filed anything or that the seller's representative automatically continues under the new ownership.

Before signing, ask the title team and, where needed, a Texas real estate attorney to clarify who will contact the board, share notices, and confirm any post-closing adjustment. If your issue is an existing delinquency rather than an open valuation dispute, start with the [Harris County back-tax guide](/blog/sell-house-back-taxes-harris-county/).

## Keep the bill and your closing estimate separate

Ask the closer to label the tax figure as an issued bill or an estimate, explain which period each party is sharing, and show how the agreement handles a later correction. Our [property tax proration guide](/blog/property-tax-proration-selling-houston-house/) explains the ordinary split; an unresolved protest adds a separate question about what happens when the number changes.

**Hypothetical:** If closing proceeds are estimated at $84,000 with the current tax figures, do not add an expected $600 from a proposed reduction. Keep the working estimate at $84,000 until the final tax figure and any seller adjustment are documented. The [Comptroller's refund guidance](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/refunds.php) describes situations where a correction can lead to an overpayment refund. The collector's treatment and any settlement between buyer and seller are separate questions.

A listing can reach more buyers and may bring a higher price; a direct offer can reduce preparation and showings but may be lower. In either case, compare written terms using the same tax assumptions.
