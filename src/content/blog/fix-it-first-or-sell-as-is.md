## Before you fix it, know your other number

A seller looks at an old roof, dated kitchen, damaged flooring, foundation concerns, or years of deferred maintenance and often assumes the first step is obvious:

**Fix it.**

But that is not necessarily the first decision.

Before you spend thousands of dollars trying to make a property easier to sell, there is another question worth answering:

**What would CashAsIs pay for it exactly as it sits?**

That is the comparison we think sellers should have *before* the contractors arrive.

## Repairs aren't free just because they may increase the price

A repair can improve a property and still fail to put the same amount of money back into the seller's pocket.

Suppose you spend money on:

- roofing;
- foundation work;
- flooring;
- paint;
- plumbing;
- electrical work;
- landscaping;
- appliances;
- cleanout; or
- cosmetic updates.

The relevant question isn't simply whether the property may sell for more afterward.

It's:

**How much did you spend, how long did it take, what did you carry during that time, and how much more did you actually keep at closing?**

That is a very different calculation.

## The project can grow after it starts

Repairs have a way of uncovering more repairs.

A contractor opens a wall and finds plumbing.

Flooring comes up and exposes another problem.

A small roof repair becomes a larger roof issue.

An inspection identifies items nobody planned for.

The original budget can change quickly.

That doesn't mean repairing a property is always a bad decision. It means the decision should be made with your eyes open—and preferably with another option already in hand.

## Time has a cost too

Even when the repair budget stays on target, the calendar keeps moving.

During a renovation or market-preparation period, an owner may still be paying some combination of:

- mortgage payments;
- property taxes;
- insurance;
- utilities;
- lawn care;
- HOA dues;
- maintenance; and
- security or vacancy-related expenses.

There is also the owner's time: meeting contractors, checking work, making selections, handling delays, and solving the next problem.

A higher future sale price has to overcome more than the contractor invoice.

It has to overcome the **whole cost of getting there**.

## This is why CashAsIs starts before the repairs

We built CashAsIs so the seller can start at a different point.

Not:

**Repair → clean → prepare → market → wait → negotiate.**

But:

**Show us the property → get an as-is number → compare your options.**

You can still decide to repair and list afterward.

Getting a CashAsIs offer doesn't take that option away.

It simply gives you a real alternative before you commit more money to the property.

## Sometimes fixing it first makes sense

We aren't going to pretend every seller should choose an as-is sale.

If a property needs relatively inexpensive improvements, you have the time and cash to complete them, and your priority is pursuing the highest possible retail price, fixing the property and listing it may make sense.

That's a legitimate option.

But compare it correctly.

Don't compare:

**Future retail price vs. today's cash offer.**

Compare:

**What you realistically keep after repairs, preparation, selling costs, carrying time, concessions, and uncertainty**

against

**what you can receive from an as-is sale without doing that work first.**

Our guide on [why the sale price isn't always what you keep](/your-price-isnt-your-take-home/) goes deeper into that calculation.

## Sometimes selling as-is makes more sense

The balance can shift toward an as-is sale when:

- the repairs are substantial;
- you don't want to manage contractors;
- the property is inherited or vacant;
- you live somewhere else;
- there are already other issues competing for your attention;
- timing matters;
- you don't want to put more cash into the property;
- the property has already failed to sell; or
- you simply value certainty and simplicity more than another project.

There doesn't have to be a dramatic reason.

**Not wanting to renovate a property you already plan to sell is a reason.**

There isn't one answer for every property.

**1. Know what the property needs.**

**2. Know what those repairs may really cost.**

**3. Know what CashAsIs would pay without those repairs.**

**4. Compare the full outcome.**

Then choose the route that makes the most sense for you.
