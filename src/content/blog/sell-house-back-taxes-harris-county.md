## The short answer

**Yes. A Harris County house with back taxes may still be sold if the delinquent amounts and other title requirements can be satisfied through the closing.**

Start with the latest tax notice you have and note any lawsuit, hearing, judgment, payment plan, or deadline. The actual amount due should be confirmed rather than estimated from an old bill. CashAsIs can evaluate the property's condition while tax and title figures are being assembled.

Do not assume that requesting an offer pauses a government or court deadline. If a date is approaching, tell us and seek appropriate professional guidance. For more context, see [our Houston back-tax guide](/blog/sell-house-behind-on-taxes-houston/) and [Harris County selling guide](/blog/sell-house-fast-harris-county-tx/).

## Build a simple payoff picture before guessing at your proceeds

Start with the latest property-tax statement or notice you have, then add any mortgage payoff, known liens, and expected closing obligations. The exact figures still need to be confirmed through the proper closing process, but this gives the seller a clearer framework: property value minus legitimate payoffs and transaction costs equals the money potentially available after closing. That is more useful than looking only at the headline sale price.

## Deadlines deserve attention even while you evaluate a sale

If your correspondence mentions a lawsuit, hearing, judgment, sale date, or other deadline, do not assume that requesting an offer pauses it. Share the timing with CashAsIs and consult the appropriate tax, title, or legal professional when needed. We can evaluate the Harris County property in its current condition, but external deadlines and legal requirements continue on their own schedule unless the responsible authority changes them.
