## Every month you keep it, the property keeps costing something

A property doesn't stop costing money just because nobody is living there.

If you own a vacant property, second property, former rental, or a house you simply aren't using anymore, you may still be paying for it month after month.

**Taxes. Insurance. Maintenance. Utilities. Lawn care. Repairs.**

One expense may not seem overwhelming by itself.

But together—and over time—they can turn an unused property into an expensive obligation.

## Property taxes don't stop

Whether the property is occupied or empty, property taxes continue.

If you're holding a property you no longer need, every additional tax period is another cost attached to an asset that may not be producing anything for you.

And if taxes become delinquent, penalties, interest, collection activity, or other complications may increase the burden.

## Insurance can become more complicated

Vacant and non-owner-occupied properties may have different insurance considerations than a primary residence.

Whatever coverage applies to your property, the important point is simple:

**You're paying to protect a property you're not actively using.**

That carrying cost continues for as long as you own it.

## Maintenance doesn't disappear when you leave

An empty property still needs attention.

Grass grows. Trees need trimming. Plumbing can leak. Roofs age. HVAC systems fail. Pests get inside. Weather causes damage.

Small issues can become larger repairs when nobody is regularly at the property to notice them.

If you live somewhere else, you may also be paying someone just to check on the property or handle basic maintenance.

## Add up the real monthly cost

Don't look at only one bill.

Consider everything you're spending because you still own the property:

- property taxes;
- insurance;
- utilities;
- lawn and exterior maintenance;
- HOA charges, if applicable;
- repairs;
- security or monitoring;
- travel to check on the property; and
- your own time managing it.

Then multiply that by another **3, 6, or 12 months**.

That's the real cost of waiting.
