## The sooner you act, the more room you have before a foreclosure sale

When a foreclosure sale date is approaching, **time becomes part of the transaction**.

If selling the property is one of the options you are considering, starting earlier gives you more time to see what the property can sell for, review an offer, make a decision, and complete the work required for closing.

Two weeks remaining is very different from two days remaining.

## An offer is only the beginning

Receiving a purchase offer does not mean the property is already sold. After an agreement is signed, a closing may still require time for:

- title review;
- an official mortgage payoff;
- taxes, liens, or judgments that appear on title;
- signatures from everyone who must sign;
- heirship, probate, divorce, bankruptcy, or ownership issues when applicable; and
- final closing, funding, and recording.

Many transactions are straightforward. Others uncover something that takes additional time to resolve.

**Starting earlier gives everyone more room to deal with those issues before the scheduled sale date.**

## Confirm the date and ask whether more time is available

If you have received foreclosure notices, confirm the scheduled sale date with your mortgage servicer.

You can also ask the servicer whether any available foreclosure-prevention option, adjustment, postponement, or additional time applies to your loan and circumstances. Do not assume a scheduled sale has changed unless the appropriate party confirms it.

Even when you are pursuing another option, knowing the actual deadline helps you make decisions with better information.

## If selling is an option, get a real number early

You do not have to wait until you are certain you want to sell before finding out what an as-is cash offer could look like.

Getting an offer earlier gives you time to:

1. see the actual number;
2. compare it with your payoff and other options;
3. decide whether the offer works for you; and
4. leave enough time for title and closing if you choose to move forward.

Waiting until the last few days can remove flexibility that you had earlier.
