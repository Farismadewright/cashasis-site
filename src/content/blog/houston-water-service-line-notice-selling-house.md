**If you receive a water-service-line material notice before selling a Houston house, identify the provider and the exact inventory category before judging what the notice means for your sale.** Keep the notice, a dated inventory lookup and any supporting inspection or plumbing records together. Do not treat an unknown material as either confirmed lead or proof that the line is non-lead.

The seller's immediate task is to describe the evidence accurately. A notice is not, by itself, a repair quote, a water-quality test or a determination of what a particular purchase agreement requires.

## Confirm that you are using the right provider's records

Start with the property's water bill or other verified service information. A Houston mailing address does not automatically mean the City is the retail water provider. Properties in the broader Harris and Fort Bend area may have another public system, a utility district or a private well arrangement.

For City customers, [Houston Public Works' service-line inspection page](https://houstonpublicworks.org/water-service-line-inspections) describes its work to identify customer-side materials across six distribution systems. The page links to the City's inventory and instructions for reporting material information.

Use the provider's current official page rather than a screenshot someone forwarded without a date. If the lookup seems to identify a neighboring property or the wrong service, ask the provider to confirm the record before using it in a buyer conversation. This guide makes no statement about an individual property's pipe material.

## Separate the inventory from testing and repair history

The [EPA's inventory guidance](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/planning-and-developing-service-line-inventory) identifies service-line categories including lead, galvanized requiring replacement, non-lead and lead status unknown. It also distinguishes system-side and customer-side classifications where ownership is split.

Keep each category and side as actually reported. Do not combine different labels into one unsupported description of every pipe serving the house.

Organize three types of records:

1. **Material information:** What the provider's inventory or inspection says about the service line.
2. **Water testing:** Any actual laboratory report, its date and what was tested.
3. **Work history:** Invoices, permits or other documents showing the scope of plumbing work performed.

A statement that plumbing was replaced years ago may be incomplete. Ask what portion was replaced and what documentation exists. Equally, a material category should not be described as a current sample result. If you have health or water-quality questions, follow the provider's current guidance and obtain appropriate professional advice; this article does not provide a testing or treatment plan.

## Build a small seller records file

Save the original notice or door hanger and note when it was received. Add a dated inventory result, any provider correspondence and the documents you already have for relevant work. Preserve the exact wording of unresolved categories in your private notes.

Houston Public Works explains that its contractors leave material information after an inspection and provide information about reporting material where it remains unknown. Use those official instructions or ask the provider for help. Do not dig, disturb a pipe or guess at a visual identification because you feel pressure to finish a sale file.

If the provider has not verified a detail, mark it unresolved. If a contractor recommends work, ask for the location, scope, assumptions and price in writing. A general phrase such as “bad plumbing” is not enough to tell whether the recommendation concerns the service line, interior plumbing or another system.

## Keep this distinct from lead paint and private wells

A lead-paint record concerns painted surfaces and related housing requirements; it does not identify service-line material. Our [older-house lead-paint guide](/blog/selling-pre-1978-houston-house-lead-paint/) explains that separate paperwork question.

Likewise, the [private-well records guide](/blog/selling-houston-area-house-private-water-well/) concerns a different water-supply arrangement. Do not apply a City service-line lookup to a house that is actually supplied by its own well.

For the sale disclosure conversation, review the known information and records with the appropriate real-estate professional or your attorney. Our [Texas as-is disclosure checklist](/blog/texas-as-is-sale-seller-disclosure-checklist/) is a starting point for organizing that discussion, not permission to omit an unresolved issue.

## Compare sale choices without inventing a repair allowance

You can compare an as-is cash proposal with listing the property while the document question is being clarified. A listed sale may offer broader exposure and a higher price; a direct proposal may reduce preparation and showing demands but can be lower.

Use what is actually known in the comparison. Do not assume that an unknown category establishes a replacement cost, that every buyer will require the same work or that a cash purchase removes legitimate requirements.

When contacting Cash As-Is, describe the property, its actual water provider and the notice or records available. A useful offer discussion separates the home's condition, the evidence still being checked and the responsibilities in the proposed agreement. It does not turn a material inventory into a water-quality guarantee.
