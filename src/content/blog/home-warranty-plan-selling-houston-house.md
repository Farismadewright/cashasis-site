**Before promising that a home warranty will go with your Houston house, locate the actual residential service contract and ask its provider what happens when ownership changes.** Keep the plan's coverage, an appliance's current condition and the terms of the house sale in separate conversations.

For a Harris County or Fort Bend seller, a brochure saying the house has protection is not enough to explain what a buyer would receive. The useful next step is a short document review, not automatically renewing, canceling or buying another plan.

## Identify which kind of warranty you have

The [Texas Real Estate Commission's home-warranty guidance](https://www.trec.texas.gov/residential-service-companies) describes residential service contracts and distinguishes them from homeowners insurance. It identifies the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation as the licensing agency for these providers. This is a service-plan question, rather than an insurance policy or a promise that the house is free of defects.

Find the provider name, the complete contract, its current term and any amendments. An appliance manufacturer's warranty, a contractor's repair warranty and a residential service plan are different documents. Do not describe one as the other simply because each uses the same word.

For a particular appliance problem, our [AC replacement decision guide](/blog/replace-air-conditioner-before-selling-houston-house/) helps separate condition findings and project scope. A service plan does not supply that diagnosis.

## Read the limits before describing the benefit

TDLR's [January 2025 residential service contract guide](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/media/pdf/consumer-protection-rsc-home-warranty-2025.pdf) recommends reviewing what is covered, exclusions, payment limits, service-call charges and repair arrangements. Use the actual plan to answer those questions; do not infer coverage from a monthly payment or an advertising headline.

Prepare a plain-language note for your own sale file:

- What document and version have you located?
- Which items are listed, and what remains unclear?
- Is the plan currently active, and who confirmed that?
- What has the provider said in writing about the ownership change?
- Which promises, if any, are included in the proposed sale agreement?

Record an unanswered question as unanswered. Do not convert an open request into approved work or describe a repair as completed before you have evidence of completion.

## Resolve the ownership-change question directly

Ask the provider to point to the actual transfer, cancellation or other ownership-change provision in your agreement. Request confirmation of any action, remaining term or charge that applies. This guide does not promise that your plan can transfer, that cancellation produces a refund or that every company uses the same procedure.

Keep the provider's answer with the sale documents. If the buyer wants a different arrangement, ask the appropriate real-estate professional or attorney to explain the written responsibilities before agreeing. A buyer's preference and an existing provider's approval are separate matters.

Texas regulator guidance says buying a residential service contract is not generally required when purchasing a home. That does not decide what two parties have actually agreed in a particular transaction. Cash As-Is terms should be read on their own; do not substitute assumptions about another contract form.

## Keep an open service request visible

**Hypothetical example:** A seller has an active plan and a pending request for a malfunctioning appliance. Instead of advertising that the appliance will be replaced, the seller identifies the request as pending and asks the provider what has actually been approved. The sale conversation then uses the known condition and the written response, without promising an outcome.

If the response is unclear or disputed, TDLR's consumer guide identifies an RSC Ombudsman as a resource for communication problems involving residential service contracts. A request for help is not a guarantee of coverage or a closing date.

An [open homeowners insurance claim](/blog/selling-houston-house-open-insurance-claim/) is a different matter. Avoid combining an insurer's possible payment and a service-plan benefit into one assumed credit in your sale proceeds.

## Compare the sale using confirmed responsibilities

Listing may provide wider exposure and a higher price, while requiring preparation, showings and negotiation over condition. An as-is cash offer may reduce preparation and showing demands but can be lower. A service plan does not prove that either route will net more.

Use the [net-proceeds comparison](/compare-the-net/) with clearly labeled assumptions, and keep unresolved plan questions out of the confirmed column. When requesting a Cash As-Is evaluation, describe the property's condition and any pending service question. Share private plan records through an appropriate channel, rather than posting account details in a public listing or message.
