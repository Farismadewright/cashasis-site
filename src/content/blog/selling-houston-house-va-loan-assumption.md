**If a buyer wants to assume your VA loan, contact the servicer before you commit to a closing date. Ask what the buyer needs to qualify, what written release you will receive, and whether your VA entitlement can be restored.** Those are separate questions. A buyer's promise to take over the payments does not answer any of them.

## Confirm who approves the assumption

Start with the company that services your current mortgage. Ask who handles the application, when the file is considered complete, and what proof of approval and release you will get at closing. In its [lender handbook on loan processing](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000314666/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-05-How-to-Process-VA-Loans-and-Submit-them-to-VA), VA explains that the current holder or servicer handles the request, with VA prior approval when required. VA's [assumption guidance](https://www.benefits.va.gov/HOMELOANS/documents/circulars/26-23-10.pdf) covers a current loan, the buyer's agreement to assume full liability, and creditworthiness.

If you are selling in Harris or Fort Bend County, give your title team the servicer's assumption instructions early. Ask the closer to identify which paperwork they are waiting on and what could change the proposed closing date. If a buyer proposes an informal payment takeover instead of the approved process, get independent legal advice before agreeing to transfer ownership.

## Get separate answers about liability and entitlement

A release addresses whether you remain responsible for the assumed loan. Entitlement is the VA benefit used to back that loan and can affect a later VA purchase. Ask the servicer for separate written answers:

- **Release:** What confirms I am released, and when does that take effect?
- **Entitlement:** Is the buyer eligible and willing to substitute sufficient entitlement, and what will VA need to document that?

A buyer can qualify for an assumption without being a veteran. Without a qualifying substitution, the portion of the seller's entitlement used for that loan generally stays tied to it until payoff. The [VA eligibility FAQ](https://www.benefits.va.gov/homeloans/eligibility.asp) explains why some sellers may still have remaining entitlement even if the used portion has not been restored. If you plan to buy again with a VA loan, confirm the effect with VA or a lender before counting on that benefit.

## Check the buyer's gap and compare your net

**Hypothetical, before fees or credits:** At a $250,000 price with a $190,000 loan balance to assume, the buyer needs a plan for the $60,000 difference. That gap is not your final take-home. Ask the closing team to verify the assumed balance, any buyer financing, and the seller charges on an itemized net sheet. If you compare with a sale that pays off the loan, use the dated payoff quote rather than assuming it is equal to the balance.

Listing with an assumption option may appeal to a buyer who values the existing loan terms, but approval and the gap can limit who can close. Listing with an ordinary payoff may open the sale to other buyers. A direct cash offer with a payoff can avoid assumption processing and reduce preparation, though it may be lower.
