**You may be able to sell a Houston house for its owner using a power of attorney, but have the actual authority reviewed before promising a sale or closing date.** The document's title alone does not establish what you can sign or whether it fits this transaction.

For a family member helping a living homeowner, the useful first step is a document review with the Texas real-estate attorney and closing team. Keep the owner's decisions, your role and the property's ownership records clear. A buyer's willingness to purchase does not settle those questions.

## Separate authority from signing convenience

The [Texas State Law Library's power-of-attorney guide](https://guides.sll.texas.gov/powers-of-attorney) explains that a written power of attorney authorizes an agent to act for a principal. Different types grant different powers. Do not assume that a document used for medical decisions, a vehicle transaction or another narrow purpose also authorizes a house sale.

Start by asking what the document actually permits, whether it remains effective and what the closing team needs to evaluate it. Do not fill gaps by signing as though you were the owner.

An owner being away from Houston is a different question. The owner may have a closing-approved way to sign personally. Our [out-of-state seller signing guide](/blog/selling-houston-house-from-out-of-state-signing/) covers those logistics. A power of attorney should not become a shortcut chosen merely because mailing documents feels inconvenient.

## Get the property and document reviewed together

[TexasLawHelp's power-of-attorney guidance](https://texaslawhelp.org/article/powers-of-attorney), provided by the Legal Hotline for Texans, discusses special authority for real-property transactions, the property description and recording considerations. Bring the actual document and property records to the professionals handling the sale.

Ask them to confirm these points rather than deciding validity from an online checklist:

- Does the authority cover the proposed transaction and the person who will sign?
- Does the property information match the house being sold?
- Are supporting documents, certifications or recording steps needed?
- Who will confirm the acceptable signature format and closing requirements?

Send sensitive documents through a verified channel. The article is not a POA form, legal opinion or finding that a particular document must be accepted.

## Build a short authority-review packet

Gather the complete document, including all pages and attachments, the owner's name as shown in ownership records and the property identification available in existing closing documents. Note who prepared the authority and whom the closing team should contact for questions.

Keep three lists: information already supplied, questions awaiting professional review and steps that must occur before signing. Request a named next action for every unresolved item. That is more useful than repeatedly asking whether everything is “fine.”

If the owner has died or circumstances have changed, stop assuming the earlier authority is sufficient. Ask a Texas attorney what authority now applies. The [multiple-owner and heir guide](/blog/sell-house-multiple-owners-heirs-houston/) concerns a different ownership situation; it is not a substitute for that advice.

## Resolve the signing plan before arranging the move

Consider a hypothetical daughter assisting a living parent who owns a Harris County house. She has a document called a power of attorney and can arrange property access. Those facts alone do not establish her authority to sign the sale papers. She first sends the document for review, then coordinates the approved signing and access plan.

Keep that order even when the house is vacant or needs repairs. If the review identifies an issue, bring it to the attorney or closing professional promptly. Do not alter dates or create your own replacement wording to keep a promised closing on track.

## Compare the sale choices separately

A listing can provide broader market exposure when the owner can accommodate preparation, showings and the selling process. A current-condition Cash As-Is offer may be useful when the owner prefers to discuss repairs and access without first preparing for a retail listing. Compare price, responsibilities and the confirmed closing plan.
