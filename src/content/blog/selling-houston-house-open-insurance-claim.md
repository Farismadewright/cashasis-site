**An open insurance claim deserves its own review before you agree to sell your Houston house.** You can explore selling, but do not assume that the buyer's price and the insurer's repair estimate are two unrestricted payments you can simply add together.

The useful first step is to separate three questions: What is the house worth in its current condition? What has the insurer actually agreed to pay? Who controls that money, and what conditions remain?

This guide helps organize that conversation. It does not determine coverage, assign claim rights or interpret your mortgage or purchase agreement.

## A repair estimate is not the same as money available to you

The Texas Department of Insurance explains that [replacement-cost home claims may involve two payments](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/getting-your-insurance-claim-paid.html). An initial payment accounts for depreciation; another payment can follow after repairs. If there is a mortgage, the mortgage company may also be involved in releasing funds for the work.

That matters when someone proposes buying the damaged house before repairs are finished. The insurance estimate may describe the cost of restoring the property, while the offer describes buying it in its present condition. Neither document alone answers what happens to unpaid claim money.

TDI's [home insurance guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb025.html) also distinguishes replacement-cost coverage from actual-cash-value coverage. Review the policy and settlement explanation rather than assuming every claim follows the same payment arrangement.

## Build a claim-and-sale checklist before comparing offers

Use copies of the records you already have. Share sensitive policy and loan information through an appropriate secure channel with the professionals who need it.

<div style="overflow-x:auto" role="region" aria-label="Open insurance claim and house sale checklist" tabindex="0">

| Item to confirm | Who to ask | Why it changes your decision |
|---|---|---|
| Accepted, disputed and still-pending damage | Insurer or adjuster | An estimate is not confirmation of every requested payment |
| Payments already issued and named payees | Insurer and mortgage servicer | A check may require more than your signature |
| Conditions on any unpaid amount | Insurer | Repair-dependent money should not be treated as certain cash |
| Funds currently held for repairs | Mortgage servicer | Ask how the proposed sale affects their handling |
| Unfinished work and contractor agreements | Contractor and transaction adviser | Identify commitments before promising a clean handoff |
| Treatment of the claim in the proposed sale | Insurer and qualified transaction advisers | Put responsibilities in writing before closing |

</div>

Ask for an explanation you can keep with your sale paperwork. If answers conflict, resolve the conflict before using the disputed amount to compare your options.

### Keep two separate columns in your estimate

For planning, make one column for the proposed sale: price, mortgage payoff and other seller items. Make another for the claim: payments received, funds held and amounts still conditional. Mark unresolved entries as unknown.

This is a decision tool, not an accounting or legal determination. Its value is showing exactly which unanswered question could change your expected take-home amount. Our [seller-net comparison guide](/compare-the-net/) can help with the sale side of that exercise.

## Repair first or request an as-is offer?

Repairing and listing may make sense if the claim arrangement is clear, you can manage the work and the likely market result justifies the time and remaining costs. Get a realistic repair scope and a listing opinion before assuming a full renovation will pay for itself.

An as-is proposal may be useful when coordinating contractors, payments and a sale would be difficult. The buyer's offer should account for the property's current condition. It should not depend on a verbal assurance that insurance will make everyone whole.

For the property-condition side, see our [Houston fire-damage selling guide](/blog/sell-fire-damaged-house-houston/). For the broader choice, compare [cash offers and listing](/blog/cash-offer-vs-listing-house-houston/). Damage type and insurance coverage are separate questions; a house being damaged does not establish that a policy covers it.

## What to tell Cash As-Is when you request a review

Explain the visible damage, whether repairs have begun, and whether an insurance claim remains unresolved. You do not need to present a polished house or guess at the final insurance outcome.

Confirm claim rights and payment requirements with the insurer, mortgage servicer and an appropriate adviser before accepting terms that depend on them. A clear offer comparison starts with knowing which money is confirmed and which questions are still open.
