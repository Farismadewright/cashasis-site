## You don't have to live across the country to be an absentee owner

Maybe you live in another state.

Maybe you live across Houston.

Or maybe you simply don't live in the neighborhood where the property sits.

The issue is the same: **you're not there every day to see what's happening.**

When a property looks vacant and the owner isn't regularly around, people can notice.

Most people will keep walking.

Some may see an opportunity to take advantage.

## Vacant properties can attract squatters and unauthorized occupants

A house that appears empty can become a target for unauthorized entry.

Signs can include:

- broken or forced doors and windows;
- unfamiliar vehicles;
- changed locks;
- belongings appearing inside;
- utilities being used unexpectedly; or
- neighbors noticing people coming and going.

The longer unauthorized occupancy goes unnoticed, the more complicated the situation may become.

Regularly checking the property—or having someone trustworthy nearby who can—is important while you continue to own it.

## Damage can grow before you even know it happened

Distance also makes ordinary property problems harder to catch.

A leak, storm-damaged roof, broken window, vandalism, illegal dumping, overgrown yard, or code issue may exist for days or weeks before the owner hears about it.

By the time someone sends you a photo, what started as a small issue may already be expensive.

## Property fraud and forged paperwork are real risks

Property owners should also pay attention to unexpected documents, filings, mail, or communications involving their real estate.

Fraud involving real property can include forged signatures, fraudulent deeds, false identities, or attempts to create the appearance that someone else has authority over a property.

That does **not** mean every vacant property is likely to experience fraud.

It means an owner who isn't nearby should be especially careful about monitoring the property and its records rather than assuming nothing can happen while the house sits empty.

If you discover suspicious paperwork or believe someone has forged a document involving your property, contact the appropriate county office and qualified legal counsel promptly.

## You shouldn't have to manage the property forever

Owning a property you don't use can start feeling like a second job.

Someone still has to coordinate:

- lawn care;
- repairs;
- contractors;
- taxes and insurance;
- utilities;
- security;
- code notices;
- access to the property; and
- periodic trips or check-ins.

If you're already tired of managing it, continuing to hold the property may simply create more opportunities for something else to go wrong.
