**You may be able to sell a Houston house with an FHA partial claim, but check for its separate payoff before estimating what you will receive.** A current first-mortgage statement may not answer the whole debt question. Ask your mortgage servicer and closing team to identify the assistance documents and the amount needed for the proposed sale.

This guide concerns an FHA loss-mitigation partial claim. It is not about an insurance claim for house damage or the separate rules for reverse mortgages. It provides general preparation steps, not a determination of your loan terms or eligibility for mortgage relief.

## Why assistance can leave another payoff to check

[HUD's explanation of FHA loss mitigation](https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/fha-loss-mitigation) describes a standalone partial claim as an interest-free subordinate lien used to address past-due mortgage amounts. HUD identifies a sale as one of the events that can make repayment due. Assistance that helped restore the payment schedule should therefore not be assumed to have forgiven the underlying amount.

Find the actual documents from the assistance arrangement. A familiar monthly payment, a current account or a recollection that the arrears were moved to the back does not establish the amount required to sell. Ask which obligations are separate and which are already reflected in a quoted total.

## Ask the closing team to reconcile both obligations

Tell the title or closing professional about the assistance early, even if you cannot locate every page. The goal is one complete debt picture, with no missing balance and no duplicate deduction.

Use a verified secure channel for loan documents. Do not put account numbers or identification in a public offer comment or send them through an unexpected message link. Ask for a clear explanation of unresolved items rather than accepting a verbal assurance that everything is included.

## A hypothetical missing-payoff example

Suppose an owner is considering a **$180,000** offer. Assume a first-loan payoff of **$135,000**, a separate partial claim of **$16,000**, and **$4,000** in other seller deductions. These are invented arithmetic inputs, not a Cash As-Is offer, typical Houston closing costs or a loan quote.

The estimate would be $180,000 − $135,000 − $16,000 − $4,000 = **$25,000**. Leaving out the partial claim would show $41,000 instead, overstating the estimate by $16,000.

The lesson is to reconcile every obligation before deciding whether the sale meets your needs. If an actual quote already incorporates a balance, do not subtract it again. If verified sale proceeds will not cover the required payments and costs, ask the servicer and closing professionals what approved options exist; do not assume an ordinary sale can proceed unchanged.

## Use HUD's current servicing route, not an old payment instruction

HUD publishes its [Secretary-held mortgage servicing information](https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/mortgage-servicing-contractors), including the route for partial-claim payoff documentation and authorized third-party requests. Let the people handling your closing confirm the appropriate request and authorization process.

The same HUD page warns that Secretary-held lien payoff instructions containing wire-transfer directions are fraudulent. Treat that specific warning seriously. Have the closing team verify the actual obligation and current official payment instructions; this article is not a place to copy bank details or improvise a payment.

## Compare a sale with the option of keeping the house

If staying is your preference, speak with the servicer about the applicable options before assuming a sale is necessary. HUD's guidance describes home-retention programs and access to HUD-approved housing counseling; whether a particular option fits requires an individual review.

If selling is the goal, compare the expected net, condition requirements and workable timeline of each proposal. Listing may provide broader buyer exposure. An as-is cash evaluation may suit a house needing work or an owner seeking fewer preparation tasks. Neither route cancels a partial claim or guarantees a completion date.
