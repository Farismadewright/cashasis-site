## Sell while you still control the decision

Back property taxes can start as a bill and become a much bigger property problem if they remain unresolved.

Depending on the circumstances, delinquent taxes can lead to additional penalties and interest, collection activity, a tax suit, judgment, and potentially a tax foreclosure sale.

**If selling is already something you are considering, it can make sense to find out what the property could sell for before the timeline gets tighter.**

## Waiting does not make the tax balance disappear

As time passes, applicable penalties, interest, fees, or collection costs can increase the amount connected to the property.

And once legal proceedings begin, there may be additional deadlines and requirements to deal with.

The exact process varies, but one thing is consistent: **waiting usually does not create more options.**

## A tax suit or judgment changes the situation

If you have received tax-suit papers, a judgment, or notice of a scheduled sale, the issue has moved beyond simply having a past-due tax bill.

At that point, timing becomes especially important.

A buyer making an offer is only the beginning of a sale. Title still needs to review the property, determine the tax balance and other required payoffs, obtain necessary documents and signatures, and complete closing and funding.

## Selling can give you another path

If the property has enough value to cover the amounts that must be addressed, a sale may allow applicable delinquent taxes and other required payoffs to be handled through closing.

A simple way to think about it is:

**GET AN OFFER → HANDLE APPLICABLE TAXES & PAYOFFS AT CLOSING → MOVE FORWARD**

You do not need to pay the delinquent taxes first just to get a CashAsIs offer.

## Find out what the property could sell for now

CashAsIs buys properties **as-is**.

You do not need to repair, clean, stage, or prepare the property for a traditional listing before we make an offer.

Getting the number does not obligate you to sell. It tells you whether a sale could make sense while you still have the ability to make that decision.

## Don't wait until the decision is made for you

If there is already a tax suit, judgment, or scheduled tax sale, do not assume there will always be more time.

Confirm important deadlines with the appropriate party and take legal or tax advice where needed.

But if selling is one of the paths you are considering, **find out what the property could sell for now rather than waiting until there may not be enough time to complete a transaction.**
