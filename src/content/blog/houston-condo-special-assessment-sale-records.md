**If you are selling a Houston condo with a special assessment, separate the regular dues, amounts currently unpaid and future association projects before comparing offers.** A recent payment receipt alone is not a complete picture of the costs or documents affecting your sale.

The useful first step is a current association-record review with the closing team. Do not assume a buyer takes every charge, that every planned project is already your personal debt or that a cash sale makes the assessment disappear. The documents and actual agreement need their own review.

## Confirm that you need condominium documents

The [Texas Real Estate Commission's condominium resale-certificate page](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/condominium-resale-certificate-0) identifies a certificate prepared and signed by the condominium association. It is different from the non-condominium association paperwork discussed in our [Texas house HOA document guide](/blog/hoa-resale-documents-texas-house-sale/).

A townhouse-style appearance does not answer the legal classification question. Ask the closing professional to confirm the property type before ordering documents based on what a neighboring seller used.

This article does not require Cash As-Is sellers to use a particular TREC purchase contract. The certificate provides a useful view of association information; the appropriate documents and responsibilities depend on the transaction.

## Read separate cost categories separately

The [official condominium certificate](https://www.trec.texas.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-forms/32-5.pdf) has distinct fields for periodic common expenses, unpaid common or special assessments, other seller balances, approved capital expenditures, reserves and transfer-related costs. Those categories answer different questions.

<div style="overflow-x:auto" role="region" aria-label="Condo seller cost questions" tabindex="0">

| Information in your file | Question for the reviewer |
|---|---|
| Regular dues statement | What recurring payment is current? |
| Assessment notice and payment history | What is approved, due, paid or still disputed? |
| Project or budget information | What planned work needs explanation? |
| Transfer-related charges | What costs appear in this proposed transaction? |
| Closing estimate | How were the confirmed items reflected in the proceeds? |

</div>

Do not add every number on every page together. Ask whether an amount is already included elsewhere and which information remains an estimate. Equally, do not omit a notice simply because you are waiting for clarification.

## Gather the notice before making a net-proceeds promise

Provide the actual assessment correspondence, available payment records and association-management contact through the closing team's verified channel. Ask who obtains the current certificate and supporting material, and who will reconcile any mismatch.

A useful review log has four columns: question, supporting document, responsible person and next confirmed step. For example, “assessment balance differs from my last receipt” is a clear issue to resolve. “Everything is paid” without supporting records is harder to evaluate.

For disputed amounts, deadlines or legal rights, obtain property-specific advice from a Texas attorney. Do not stop paying or promise a waiver based on a general article. Our [Houston lien guide](/blog/sell-house-with-liens-houston/) covers broader title questions and does not decide an association dispute.

## Compare proposals using the same assumptions

Imagine a hypothetical seller comparing a listing estimate with a direct purchase proposal. One estimate includes a confirmed association charge; the other leaves it blank. The headline figures are not yet a fair comparison. Ask for both estimates to state what is included, excluded and awaiting confirmation.

An attractive proposed price does not automatically establish the amount available after closing. Ask how a later update to association information would be handled under the actual terms before treating an estimate as money you can spend.

## Choose the sale route after clarifying the records

Listing can provide wider market exposure when you can manage the preparation, showings and marketing process. A current-condition Cash As-Is evaluation may be useful when the unit needs work or you prefer to discuss a direct purchase. Compare price, responsibilities and confirmed timing rather than assuming either route always nets more.

Tell us the condo's location, condition and any assessment review in progress. Ask whether the unit fits what we buy. An evaluation is not a promise to buy every condo, resolve an association dispute or bypass required paperwork. Clear records make the conversation more practical without requiring you to guess at the association's answers.
