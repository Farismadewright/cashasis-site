**If you paid off a mortgage but it still appears in a Houston house's title review, gather the payoff evidence and ask the closing team to check the recorded release.** Do not assume you owe the loan again, and do not assume a zero-balance statement completes the title work.

This is a document-preparation guide for Houston-area sellers, with a Harris County records starting point. It does not determine whether a particular lien is enforceable or tell you how to clear it yourself. A title professional or Texas real-estate attorney should resolve the property-specific issue.

## Separate the payment question from the record question

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains how to check a mortgage lien release](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/after-i-have-paid-off-my-mortgage-how-do-i-check-if-my-lien-was-released-en-206/): consult property records and contact the company involved in the payoff. The agency also notes that recording a release may follow repayment after a delay.

For a seller, the useful distinction is between evidence that money was paid and evidence that the related release has been recorded. A statement, payoff confirmation and recorded instrument answer different questions. Keep them together rather than treating any one document as the complete answer.

If a title review flags an old mortgage, ask which recorded document is involved. A general description such as an old bank loan is too vague to guide the next call. Have the closing team identify what it needs to match or resolve.

## Build a release-review packet before scheduling around it

Start with records you already possess. Use the closing office's verified, secure delivery method for financial documents; do not post them publicly or send account details through an unexpected link.

Missing paperwork is not a reason to manufacture a document or guess at its contents. Tell the reviewer what you can locate and ask where to begin the search. If someone else handled the payoff, identify that office through your own trusted records.

## Use the correct county's records without treating a search as title clearance

The [Harris County Clerk's Real Property Document Search Portal](https://www.cclerk.hctx.net/Applications/WebSearch/RP.aspx) provides search fields including document number, recording dates and party names. Ask the closing team for the relevant recording reference when possible, rather than searching only by a familiar bank name.

The clerk cautions that online records can lag processing and may not reproduce the complete official record. A search result is a starting point for review. Failing to find a release does not, by itself, prove that nothing was recorded; finding a document with a similar name does not prove it clears your transaction.

Houston-area properties cross county lines. Confirm the property's county before using a Harris County search for a house located elsewhere, including Fort Bend County. Let the closing team identify the appropriate record and whether an official copy is needed. This guide does not require you to purchase records before requesting an offer.

## Ask for a specific next action rather than a promised closing date

Use a simple handoff: which instrument needs attention, who is obtaining the missing evidence, and when will that person provide an update? These are coordination questions, not a legal conclusion about what must be filed.

A hypothetical example: an owner has a paid-loan letter, but the title reviewer cannot yet match a recorded release to the flagged mortgage. The next useful step is sending the letter securely and requesting that matching review. It is neither automatically paying the old balance again nor promising a buyer that the issue is already cleared.

If money is requested, have the closing team explain the documented basis and verify payment instructions independently. Our [house-sale wire-fraud checklist](/blog/protect-house-sale-proceeds-wire-fraud/) covers that separate payment-security step.

## Compare the sale routes while the paperwork is reviewed

A cash purchase does not erase a recorded lien or settle a disputed title question. A listed sale also needs a workable closing process. Compare the complete written proposal, responsibilities and timing assumptions rather than assuming either route avoids the records issue.

Listing may fit a seller seeking broader market exposure who can handle preparation and showing arrangements. An as-is offer may fit someone who prefers a current-condition evaluation without first renovating the property.
