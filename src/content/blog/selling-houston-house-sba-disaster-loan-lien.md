**If an SBA disaster-loan lien appears in the paperwork for your Houston house sale, ask the servicing office and title team what must happen to release the property before promising a closing date or a take-home amount.** A loan balance, a collateral-release request and a completed payoff are different things.

This guide concerns an existing SBA disaster loan and a possible sale of real-estate collateral. It does not decide whether your property secures a loan, prescribe a short sale or apply disaster-loan procedures to every SBA business loan. For a Harris or Fort Bend property, begin with the actual recorded documents and the office servicing that particular account.

## Identify the obligation before planning around it

Make a short private list of what the title search or your own records actually show: the lender or agency name, the property referenced, the relevant loan documents and any servicing correspondence. Share sensitive copies only through the verified channels established by your servicing office and closing professionals.

Then ask three separate questions:

1. Does this obligation affect the property being sold?
2. Is the proposed transaction paying the loan in full, or requesting another treatment of the collateral?
3. What written instructions and documents must the closing team have before it can finish?

Our [Houston title-problem guide](/blog/sell-house-title-problems-houston/) provides the broader sale-preparation context. This article addresses the narrower disaster-loan servicing question rather than assuming every lien follows the same process.

## Read the current requirement letter without treating it as approval

SBA publishes a [Release of Collateral Requirement Letter](https://www.sba.gov/document/support-release-collateral-requirement-letter), updated October 6, 2025. The linked disaster-loan requirements distinguish a request package from a separate written commitment. They also distinguish a principal reduction from a full payoff and warn that a paydown does not automatically recalculate regular monthly payments.

Use that guidance to frame questions for the responsible servicing office. Do not assume that downloading a form, assembling a packet or receiving an offer means the agency has approved your transaction. The publication is not a promise that you can retain a particular share of the sale proceeds.

Ask which version and requirements apply to your account now. If the account has moved to another servicing stage, or there is an active collection, foreclosure or bankruptcy issue, tell your attorney and the closing team promptly. This checklist does not suspend any obligation or deadline.

## Build a transaction file that reviewers can reconcile

Rather than sending an unexplained folder of screenshots, organize the actual sale file into a few clearly labeled sections:

- **Property and loan:** The documents identifying the property and the obligation under review.
- **Proposed transaction:** The agreement and amendments that describe the offer being evaluated.
- **Closing figures:** The draft statement and the supporting balances used in it.
- **Open requests:** What the servicing office or title team has asked for, who is responding and what is still missing.
- **Written outcome:** Any actual decision or closing instruction received, kept separate from a request or an informal discussion.

This is an organizational framework, not a complete SBA submission checklist. Obtain the required package directly from the appropriate office. Do not change a signed document or label an unresolved amount as paid simply to make the sale file look complete.

## Check the proceeds assumption before comparing offers

A purchase price does not establish what reaches your bank account. Ask the closing team to show how each proposed payment and release condition affects the draft statement. If the SBA treatment is unresolved, keep that line unresolved rather than inventing a seller-net figure.

**Hypothetical decision example:** An owner receives two proposals. One advertises an earlier closing, but the collateral-review steps have not been confirmed. The other allows more preparation time. The owner asks both buyers how their written terms handle the outstanding dependency, then compares those terms with professional guidance. Neither proposal itself supplies agency approval.

Use the [net-proceeds comparison](/compare-the-net/) for labeled sale assumptions. Keep it separate from a servicing decision and from the actual closing statement. ## Compare the selling route after identifying the dependency

Listing may provide broader exposure and a higher price, while requiring preparation, showings and a buyer's own conditions. An as-is cash proposal may reduce renovation and showing demands but can be lower. Neither route removes a legitimate servicing or title requirement.

When asking Cash As-Is to evaluate the house, describe its condition and the unresolved document question. Keep full financial records out of public messages. A useful next step is a clear list of what is verified, what is pending and who must answer it—not a guaranteed closing date before the release path is understood.
