## Yes — but the transaction needs enough time to actually close

If your property has a scheduled foreclosure sale date, you may still be able to sell before that date.

The key is timing.

Getting an offer or signing a purchase agreement is not the finish line. **The sale needs enough time to make it through title, mortgage payoff, closing, funding, and recording before the scheduled foreclosure sale.**

That is why starting earlier matters.

## Step 1: Know the scheduled sale date

If you have received foreclosure notices, confirm the scheduled sale date with your mortgage servicer or the notice you received.

That date should be shared immediately with anyone helping with a proposed sale.

Do not assume a foreclosure sale has been postponed, extended, or canceled unless the appropriate party confirms it.

## Step 2: Review the purchase offer

If selling is one of the options you are considering, CashAsIs can review the property and make an as-is cash offer.

You can look at the actual number before deciding whether you want to move forward.

If the offer works for you, the next step is a purchase agreement so the transaction can move into title and closing.

## Step 3: Title reviews the property

After an agreement is signed, the title company reviews ownership and the items that may need to be addressed for closing.

Depending on the property, that can include:

- the mortgage payoff;
- property taxes;
- liens or judgments;
- ownership and required signatures; and
- probate, heirship, divorce, bankruptcy, or other title issues when applicable.

Some properties are straightforward. Others need additional documents or time.

## Step 4: The mortgage is handled through closing

Before closing, payoff information is obtained for the mortgage and other valid amounts that must be satisfied through the transaction.

At closing, the sale proceeds are used according to the settlement statement to handle the required payoff and closing obligations.

If money remains after the applicable payoff amounts, liens, taxes, and transaction charges, the remaining proceeds are disbursed according to the closing statement.

## Step 5: The sale has to be completed in time

This is the part that matters most when foreclosure is approaching.

**A signed contract alone does not complete the sale.**

There must still be enough time to resolve title requirements, obtain the payoff, collect signatures, fund the transaction, and complete closing.

A property with several weeks remaining may have much more room to solve an unexpected title issue than a property with only a few days left.
