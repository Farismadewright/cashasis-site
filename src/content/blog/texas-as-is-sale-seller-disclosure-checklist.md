**Selling a Texas house as-is does not automatically remove the seller's disclosure duties.** For a Houston, Harris County or Fort Bend County owner, the practical starting point is to separate the house's repair needs from the information you need to provide about it.

You may prefer a buyer who evaluates the property in its current condition. That preference does not answer which notice is required in your transaction. This guide helps you prepare the conversation; it does not determine an exemption or interpret your particular contract.

## Start with the actual Texas requirement

[Texas Property Code Section 5.008](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.5.htm#5.008) generally requires a written condition notice for residential property with no more than one dwelling unit, subject to listed exceptions. It describes the seller's knowledge when signing, allows genuinely unknown information to be identified as unknown, and distinguishes the notice from warranties and inspections.

The [Texas Real Estate Commission's current Seller's Disclosure Notice page](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/sellers-disclosure-notice) identifies Form 55-1 with an effective date of May 28, 2026. Use the current source rather than an old form saved years ago. The statute permits a substantially similar notice containing its minimum items; the TREC form is a reference, not a reason to ignore transaction-specific advice.

An estate, trust or unusual ownership situation deserves review. Do not infer an exemption merely from the words inherited, vacant, cash or as-is. Ask a Texas real estate attorney which requirements apply before relying on an exception or deciding what to sign.

## Build a condition file you can explain

You do not need to turn your first call into a technical presentation. Start a private folder and separate what you observed from what someone else reported. Then use a working sheet like this:

<div style="overflow-x:auto" role="region" aria-label="Seller condition preparation worksheet" tabindex="0">

| Working note | What to put beside it |
|---|---|
| An issue you personally observed | A plain description and where it occurred |
| Work someone performed | The receipt, report or other record you actually have |
| A statement you heard | Who can help verify it; keep it separate from your observation |
| A question you cannot answer | Mark it for review rather than filling in an optimistic guess |

</div>

This is an organizing tool, not a replacement disclosure form. Avoid upgrading a receipt into a conclusion it does not support. A document showing that work was performed is different from a professional opinion about the property's current condition.

For example, your working note might say that a stain appeared, that a contractor visited, and that you have an invoice. Those are separate facts to organize. Whether the underlying issue is resolved may call for a different document or professional explanation. Do not invent one to make the paperwork look complete.

## Keep repair decisions separate from information decisions

Before ordering work solely to prepare for a sale, write down three questions:

1. What information do I already have?
2. What point needs clarification from a qualified person?
3. What repair, if any, am I choosing to undertake before comparing offers?

The answers need not be the same. Gathering an old report is an information task. Choosing whether to renovate is a spending and sale-strategy task. Reading a contract provision is another task again.

Our [explanation of selling as-is](/blog/selling-as-is-what-it-actually-means/) describes how CashAsIs approaches current condition. Use this disclosure checklist alongside that guide, rather than treating either as a substitute for the agreement and applicable requirements.

## Compare proposals using the same honest property picture

When comparing a listing plan and a direct purchase, give each professional or buyer a consistent account of the property. Keep a dated copy of documents you provide. If a question changes your understanding, ask your attorney or agent how to address it appropriately in the transaction.

Listing may fit an owner who wants to pursue retail buyers and can manage preparation and marketing. A CashAsIs purchase may fit an owner who wants an evaluation without first making the property retail-ready. Neither choice makes accurate information unimportant. Review our [cash-versus-listing comparison](/blog/cash-offer-vs-listing-house-houston/) for the broader tradeoffs.

## Start the offer conversation with what you know

You can tell CashAsIs about the condition, occupancy and questions you still need to resolve. There is no need to describe an imperfect house as perfect to start the conversation.

Ask what information matters to evaluating the property, then keep required disclosure and closing steps on their own checklist. That gives you a clearer basis for deciding whether a direct purchase meets your needs, without promising a particular legal result or requiring you to guess about the house.
