**If you move out before your Houston house sells, ask the insurer how the change affects coverage.** An active policy, a paid premium and a signed purchase agreement are different things. None should be treated as a substitute for checking the policy's occupancy conditions.

This matters for an unused house in Harris County or Fort Bend County whether you are listing it, considering an as-is buyer or still choosing a sale route. Start the insurance conversation when the occupancy changes, rather than counting on a sale to finish before questions arise.

## Tell the insurer what is actually happening

The [Texas Department of Insurance's underwriting guide](https://tdi.texas.gov/tips/auto-and-home-policy-underwriting.html) explains that occupancy affects the kinds of policies companies may offer. Vacant, unoccupied and leased properties can raise different underwriting questions.

Describe the facts instead of choosing a convenient label:

- when the last occupant left or plans to leave;
- whether anyone still stays overnight;
- which furnishings or belongings remain;
- whether utilities are operating;
- whether repairs or renovation are underway; and
- how the house is being checked while it is unused.

Ask the insurer how **your policy** classifies that situation and what it needs from you. Keep a dated note of the conversation and request the relevant policy provision or written confirmation. A yard visit or a few belongings should not become your own invented definition of occupancy.

## Check the policy, not an internet grace period

TDI's [home insurance guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb025.html) warns that policies may exclude losses after a house has been vacant for the number of days specified in the policy. Its coverage discussion directs consumers to their policy or agent for exact terms.

Separately, TDI's [cancellation and nonrenewal guidance](https://tdi.texas.gov/tips/home-insurance-canceled-or-not-renewed.html) identifies vacancy of 60 days or more as a possible reason for nonrenewal. **That is not a universal promise of full coverage for the first 60 days.** Ask about both the current policy's loss exclusions and its future renewal.

Avoid relying on a neighbor's experience or a rule from another insurer. Your questions should be specific enough that the answer can be connected to the document you actually have.

This is a question sheet, not a policy recommendation. If a change is proposed, have the insurer explain its coverage, limits, exclusions, cost and start date. Compare the actual protection offered; do not select solely by the smallest premium.

## Put the insurance dates beside the sale dates

Create one simple calendar for the property. Note the move-out date, policy renewal date, any insurer-provided deadline, your expected sale milestones and the next review date.

If the buyer's proposed closing changes, revisit the insurance plan. Keep the decision about coverage with the insurer and the decision about transfer and funding with the closing professionals.

TDI's home insurance guide also notes that a lender generally requires insurance while money is owed on the house. If you have a mortgage, include the lender's requirements in that discussion. Do not cancel simply because a buyer has made an offer or because the property is no longer convenient to maintain.

For the broader transaction milestones, see our [closing timeline guide](/closing-timeline/). The date you hope to close and the date a sale actually completes should not be confused.

## Keep property care and insurance review connected

Ask the insurer what inspections or protective steps it expects. Then decide who can carry out the applicable instructions, how the work will be documented and what happens if that person cannot attend.

You might keep a private log of visits, photographs and reported problems. Treat it as a way to organize your own property information, not a guarantee that a claim will be covered. If you discover damage or have an open claim, ask the insurer how to proceed before assuming that a sale resolves it.

Our [guide for owners living away from a vacant property](/blog/dont-live-in-area-vacant-property-risk/) discusses the wider management burden. Coverage review is one part of that burden; lawn work or occasional visits do not replace it.
