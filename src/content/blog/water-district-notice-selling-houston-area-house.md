**If your Houston-area house is in a water district, identify the district and ask for its current purchaser notice before signing sale paperwork.** A utility bill, HOA document or familiar neighborhood name is not automatically the notice your transaction requires.

For sellers in Harris or Fort Bend County, the practical first step is assembling the right district information. This guide does not decide whether a particular property or transfer falls under a legal requirement. Have the title professional or Texas real-estate attorney confirm applicability, required delivery and any exception for your sale.

## Start with the district, not the ZIP code

The [Texas Commission on Environmental Quality's water-provider guide](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/drinkingwater/whatsmysource.html) links to tools for water-service boundaries, utility service areas and water districts. Its Water District Map Viewer links to further district information. Use these official resources as a starting point when the district name is unclear.

Then confirm the result with the district and closing team. Do not infer district membership solely from a city label, a ZIP code or what applies to a neighbor. A mailing address does not answer every service-boundary or notice question.

TCEQ also cautions that being inside a water-service boundary does not necessarily establish meter availability. That is a different issue from sale-notice preparation. If service itself is uncertain, ask the relevant provider rather than interpreting a map as a connection promise.

## Obtain the district's own notice when available

The [Texas Real Estate Commission's special-district notice guidance](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/notice-purchaser-special-taxing-or-assessment-district) explains the written notice required for property in covered districts under Texas Water Code sections 49.452 and 49.4521. TREC says to use the district's published notice when available. If it is not published, obtain the information from the district for completion of the applicable form.

Do not copy a notice from a different district or guess at its financial fields. Ask which current notice matches the property and who will verify the completed information. The closing professional should confirm whether another district or notice also needs attention.

Referring to TREC's notice guidance does not mean Cash As-Is uses the TREC resale purchase contract. The actual agreement and applicable disclosure law need their own review. A direct as-is purchase is not a blanket exemption from property-specific requirements.

## Keep district, HOA and utility paperwork in separate folders

A house can raise more than one of these questions. Resolving one does not prove the others are finished. ## Ask for a named next action before committing to a date

Send the closing team the district name you have found, the source of the information and the notice you received. Ask it to confirm the correct property match and flag missing information. Avoid sending sensitive ownership or loan documents through an unverified contact route.

Request a simple update: who is checking applicability, who obtains or reviews the notice, and what needs to happen before the next sale milestone? That makes the issue manageable without treating a checklist as legal clearance.

If you have already signed and now discover a possible missing notice, do not backdate a document or assume a later signature cures the issue. Bring the facts to the closing professional or attorney promptly. This article does not determine a buyer's rights or provide a remedy for a specific contract.
