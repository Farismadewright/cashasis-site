## Selling isn't just about the price. It's about what it takes to get there.

A traditional sale may produce a higher headline price.

But the number written at the top of a contract is not necessarily the number a seller keeps.

Before comparing a CashAsIs offer with a traditional sale, look at the **whole path to closing**:

- commissions and negotiated agent compensation;
- repairs and market preparation;
- cleanup and staging;
- seller closing expenses;
- possible buyer concessions;
- inspection and repair negotiations;
- carrying costs while the property is still yours; and
- the risk and time associated with buyer financing.

CashAsIs was built to remove many of those moving pieces.

## Start with a familiar example: the 6% selling-cost benchmark

Real estate compensation is negotiable. There is no required 6% commission.

But **6% remains a useful, familiar example for sellers comparing costs**, and recent 2026 data cited by Bankrate places average total Texas real estate commissions at about **5.88%**.

Here's what a 6% example looks like:

| Sale Price | 6% Commission Example |
| ---: | ---: |
| $200,000 | $12,000 |
| $250,000 | $15,000 |
| $300,000 | $18,000 |
| $400,000 | $24,000 |
| $500,000 | $30,000 |

On a **$300,000 sale**, that's **$18,000** before considering repairs, preparation, concessions, carrying costs, or other transaction expenses.

That doesn't mean every seller pays 6%. It means a seller should compare the **net**, not simply the advertised sale price.

## Then come the costs of getting the property ready

If the property isn't retail-ready, the owner may spend money before it ever reaches closing.

That can include:

- paint and flooring;
- roof or foundation work;
- plumbing and electrical repairs;
- landscaping;
- junk removal and cleanout;
- photography or staging preparation;
- inspection-related repairs; and
- smaller cosmetic items that add up quickly.

The amount varies dramatically by property.

The important part is simple:

**Every dollar spent getting the property ready is another dollar that has to be recovered from the eventual sale.**

With CashAsIs, you can start by showing us the property **before** doing that work.

## A traditional buyer may negotiate again after the contract

Getting under contract doesn't always end the negotiation.

A traditional buyer may inspect the property and then request repairs, credits, price reductions, or other concessions.

The seller can negotiate too, of course.

But it introduces another decision point between the accepted offer and the closing table.

With a CashAsIs purchase, our offer is made with the property's current condition in mind. The goal is not to get you under contract and then ask you to renovate the property for us.

## Buyer financing adds another moving part

A financed buyer normally has a lender involved.

That can mean underwriting, appraisal requirements, loan conditions, and a financing timeline before the buyer is ready to close.

Most financed transactions close just fine.

But if you're comparing selling methods, the distinction matters:

**CashAsIs is the buyer. We are not waiting on a traditional retail buyer to obtain a mortgage to purchase the property from you.**

That removes one major dependency from the transaction.

## What about seller closing costs?

Traditional sellers can also encounter title-related fees, escrow charges, prorations, negotiated buyer concessions and other transaction expenses depending on the contract and location.

Those costs vary.

With CashAsIs, our standard offer is structured so **CashAsIs takes care of the closing costs we agree to cover in the purchase agreement**, and we explain the numbers before closing.

Property-specific obligations are different. Existing mortgages, delinquent taxes, liens or other amounts attached to the property may still need to be paid or resolved from the transaction.

The point is transparency:

**Know what you're actually walking away with.**

## A $300,000 example: compare the route, not just the price

Imagine a property that could sell traditionally for **$300,000**.

Using the familiar 6% commission example:

**$300,000 sale price**  
**− $18,000 commission example**  
**= $282,000 before other selling costs**

Now suppose the property also needs repairs or preparation.

Add whatever applies:

**− repairs/prep**  
**− seller concessions, if negotiated**  
**− seller closing expenses**  
**− continued taxes, insurance, utilities or mortgage payments while waiting**

The exact result will be different for every seller.

That's why comparing a $300,000 retail price directly against a lower cash offer can be misleading.

The better comparison is:

**What do I realistically keep, how much more money do I have to put in, and what do I have to go through to get there?**

Then compare that with the CashAsIs number.

No.

That's why the comparison matters.

CashAsIs is not built around pretending the two selling methods are identical.
