**Before canceling flood insurance because you are selling a Houston house, ask your insurance agent whether the existing NFIP building policy can be assigned to the buyer.** Confirm the actual policy, buyer's decision and required paperwork first. An accepted offer does not establish that an insurance change has been completed.

For a Harris or Fort Bend seller, this is a separate preparation question from flood damage, a pending claim or the property's map designation. It is worth raising while the sale is being organized, rather than discovering after cancellation that nobody reviewed the available policy options.

## Identify the policy you actually have

Find the declarations page and the agent or company's trusted contact information. Ask whether the coverage is through the National Flood Insurance Program, or NFIP, and whether it covers the building. Do not apply an NFIP explanation to a different insurance product without checking with its insurer.

The [NFIP's agent FAQ](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/articles/answers-some-your-clients-most-frequently-asked-questions) explains that the building's flood policy can transfer to a new buyer when the house is sold. It separately explains that the seller cannot simply move that existing NFIP policy to a different house: the premium relates to the particular property and risk.

Treat that distinction as a question to investigate, not permission to promise that every policy transfers. The [FloodSmart consumer guidance](https://www.floodsmart.gov/flood-zones-and-maps/what-is-my-flood-zone) says a buyer moving into a higher-risk zone may be able to assume the prior owner's current NFIP policy. Ask the agent to explain the options for this buyer and this building. Do not advertise a guaranteed premium or savings based on your old bill.

## Make one written handoff list

Keep a short checklist with a responsible person and the next update for each unanswered question:

- **Existing coverage:** Which policy and insured building are being reviewed, and when does its current term end?
- **Assignment option:** Is it available here, and what does the insurer require from seller and buyer?
- **Buyer's choice:** Is the buyer pursuing that option or arranging separate coverage?
- **Effective arrangement:** What written confirmation will show the chosen change has been processed?
- **Seller's next step:** When should cancellation or another change be considered, and how will any account adjustment be explained?
- **Closing changes:** Who needs an updated instruction if the proposed completion date moves?

Share policy records through a verified private channel. Ask which pages the reviewer needs before sending financial or personal information widely. A checklist entry marked “sent” is different from a confirmed insurance change; retain the response as well as your request.

## Keep three different flood questions separate

An existing policy does not replace an accurate account of what happened to the house. Our [flood-map versus flood-history guide](/blog/houston-flood-map-vs-house-flood-history/) helps separate map information from property-specific observations and records.

If a loss claim is open, use the [open-insurance-claim seller guide](/blog/selling-houston-house-open-insurance-claim/) to organize that separate discussion. Do not assume that assigning a policy answers who receives claim proceeds, what a lender requires or which repairs are part of the sale. Those questions need their own review.

For changes to an actual policy, [FloodSmart directs owners to their insurance agent or company](https://www.floodsmart.gov/contact). The closing professional can coordinate the transaction date, while the insurer confirms the coverage arrangement. Keep both informed when plans change rather than using the purchase agreement as proof of insurance processing.

## Compare the sale without turning insurance into a promise

Listing can provide broader buyer exposure if you can manage preparation, showings and the marketing period. A current-condition Cash As-Is proposal may reduce preparation demands, but its price may reflect that convenience and the property's condition. Neither route establishes whether a policy assignment is available or guarantees a closing date.

When [requesting a Cash As-Is offer](https://www.cashasis.com/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=cta&utm_campaign=blog_cta&utm_content=flood-insurance-policy-selling-houston-house&utm_term=inline), describe the condition and desired timing, and flag that the flood-policy plan remains under review. Compare the written purchase terms while the appropriate insurance professionals confirm the handoff. Make the coverage decision from their response, rather than canceling simply because a buyer has said yes.
