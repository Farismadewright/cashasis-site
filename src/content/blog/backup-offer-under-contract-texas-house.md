**Yes, a Texas seller can sign a backup contract while a first sale is under contract, if the backup is properly made contingent on the first contract ending.** A higher price or a faster closing promise does not let you replace your first buyer. Before signing anything, verify the first contract, the backup cutoff and the way notice must be delivered.

## Know whether you have a backup offer or a signed contract

A written offer can be reviewed without treating it as a second sale. Once both parties execute a contract with the [Texas Real Estate Commission’s backup addendum, Form 11-9](https://www.trec.texas.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-forms/11-9.pdf), it is binding, subject to the stated contingency and other terms. The first contract stays in place while the contingency is unresolved. The backup can still carry obligations during that period, including the deposit arrangements specified in the documents.

If you have signed only with a broker and do not have a buyer under contract, that is a different question. Start with our [guide to cash offers while your Houston house is listed](/blog/cash-offer-while-house-listed-houston/).

## Track three dates on one page

1. **The backup’s initial effective date.** Use the actual contract to check initial earnest money, any option fee and any right the backup buyer has to terminate. Do not assume all obligations or option rights wait for the first contract to end.
2. **The cutoff in Paragraph H.** Under Form 11-9, if the first contract has not terminated by the date entered there, the backup terminates and the earnest money is refunded to the backup buyer. An extension or amendment to the first contract is not a termination. If the first deal’s timeline changes, recheck the backup cutoff instead of assuming it moved too.
3. **The date the seller delivers notice.** If the first contract terminates in time, the form calls for immediate notice to the backup buyer. For performance under the backup, delivery sets the amended effective date. Ask your agent or attorney to verify the termination, the proper delivery method, and which deadlines to calendar again. [TREC identifies Form 62-0 as a voluntary notice for this purpose](https://www.trec.texas.gov/agency-information/rules-and-laws/trec-rules/).

**Hypothetical:** A first buyer gets an extension through the 25th, while a signed backup has a cutoff on the 22nd. If the first contract still stands at that cutoff, the backup ends under those terms. The extension alone neither activates the backup nor keeps it available through the 25th.

## Compare whether a backup is worth keeping

Going back to market, once you are free to do so, may attract more buyers or a better price. A signed backup may reduce the wait if your first deal ends, but it adds its own notice, dates and terms. A cash backup could avoid a buyer’s mortgage condition and may be willing to take the house as-is, but it may pay less or still have its own inspection or termination rights.
