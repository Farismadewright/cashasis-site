## Behind on taxes does not automatically mean you cannot sell

If property taxes have fallen behind, ignoring the balance usually does not make the situation easier.

But **you may not need to come up with all of the delinquent taxes out of pocket before exploring a sale.**

If selling makes sense, the taxes can be reviewed as part of the title and closing process.

## What happens to the back taxes when a property is sold?

After a purchase agreement is signed, the title company reviews the property and determines the amounts that must be addressed to complete the transaction.

That can include:

- delinquent property taxes;
- current taxes or applicable prorations;
- mortgage or other valid lien payoffs; and
- other title requirements affecting the property.

Applicable amounts are reflected on the closing statement and can be paid from the sale proceeds at closing.

If proceeds remain after the amounts that must be paid and applicable transaction charges, the remaining seller proceeds are disbursed according to the closing statement.

## Why waiting can make the problem harder

Delinquent property taxes can involve additional interest, penalties, collection activity, or legal action depending on the circumstances.

If the property is already involved in a tax suit, tax foreclosure, or scheduled sale, timing becomes even more important.

The goal is to understand the numbers **while there is still time to make a decision**, rather than waiting until the situation becomes more difficult to resolve.
