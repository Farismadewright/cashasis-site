**Choose utility stop and start dates from a confirmed sale-and-possession plan, not simply the day you move your furniture or sign documents.** For a Houston seller, the useful goal is a documented handoff that avoids an unintended gap while keeping final bills and responsibilities clear.

There is no single shutoff date this article can prescribe for every sale. Your agreement, occupancy, remaining access and provider procedures all need to fit together.

## Confirm what still needs service at the house

Before scheduling a stop, ask the closing team and buyer which visits or checks remain and what the agreement requires. Explain whether anyone still occupies the property and when possession is expected to change.

Keep signing, funding confirmation and possession on separate lines in your plan. If a closing date changes, a utility request already scheduled for the original date may need attention too. Our [cash-sale timeline guide](/blog/cash-house-sale-timeline-houston/) explains why the actual transaction steps matter more than a general speed claim.

For a rental, do not change a tenant's service as a shortcut to possession. Confirm the lease, account responsibility and applicable requirements with the appropriate professionals. The [Houston tenant-occupied selling guide](/blog/sell-house-with-tenants-houston/) addresses that different sale situation.

## List providers instead of treating utilities as one account

Write down who supplies and bills electricity, water and any other service at the property. Use your statements and verified provider contacts. Avoid assuming the same company handles every account or that a Houston mailing address identifies the water system.

For City of Houston water-billing questions, the city's [official contact directory](https://pwehelp.houstontx.gov/contactus/) identifies its water customer-service channel. If your bill names another district or provider, start with that organization rather than sending a request to the wrong office.

Do not put account passwords, identification documents or full bill images in a public listing. Give each provider information through its verified process, and share only the necessary handoff details with the people handling the sale.

## Distinguish a mailing-address change from a service request

[Reliant's account-management guidance](https://www.reliant.com/en/residential/help-support/customer-resources/faqs/account-management) says changing the billing address changes where its bill is sent; it does not change the location receiving electricity. This is a useful example of why updating contact information is not enough.

Its [transfer instructions](https://www.reliant.com/en/moving/start-or-transfer-service/transfer-service) separately ask customers to select move-out and move-in dates and explain the final bill at the old address. Those are that provider's procedures, not a promise that every seller can use the same plan or timeline. Check your own account's requirements and obtain confirmation.

## Use a short handoff worksheet

<div style="overflow-x:auto" role="region" aria-label="Seller utility handoff worksheet" tabindex="0">

| Item | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Remaining access | Which inspections or agreed visits still need service? |
| Possession | Who will control the property, and from what confirmed date? |
| Seller account | What stop or transfer request did the provider accept? |
| Buyer arrangements | Has the buyer confirmed its own required start request? |
| Final billing | Where will statements go, and who will check them? |
| Date changes | Who will revisit requests if the sale timetable changes? |

</div>

Keep confirmation numbers and provider responses privately with your sale records. A calendar reminder is useful, but a reminder you created is not proof the provider accepted the requested date.

## If service is already off, describe the uncertainty

Tell the buyer which services are unavailable before arranging a visit. Identify whether a system has actually been evaluated or whether its condition remains unknown. A lack of power or water should not be described as a successful system test.

Do not operate damaged equipment, open utility equipment or arrange an unsafe reconnection for the sake of a sale. Ask the provider and appropriately qualified professionals what is suitable for the actual condition. For an empty property, keep this issue alongside the [vacancy-insurance checklist](/blog/vacant-house-insurance-while-selling-houston/).

## Compare the full sale plan

Listing may suit an owner who wants broad exposure and can coordinate preparation and access. A Cash As-Is evaluation may suit an owner seeking a current-condition proposal with fewer preparation tasks. Either route still needs clear account and possession arrangements.

When requesting an offer, tell Cash As-Is whether the house is occupied, which utilities are operating and who can provide authorized access. Confirm the eventual handoff with the closing team and providers rather than assuming a cash purchase automatically closes every account.
