**An HCAD value and a cash offer are useful information, but they do not answer exactly the same question.** Before accepting or rejecting a Houston-area offer because it differs from the tax record, check the value label, its date, the house's current condition and what each sale option would actually leave you.

A difference deserves an explanation. It does not automatically prove the county is wrong, the buyer is right, or that you should spend money renovating.

## Start with the label and valuation date

[Harris Central Appraisal District's explanation of its notices](https://hcad.org/hcad-help/protests-and-corrections/ifile-and-isettle) distinguishes market value from appraised value. The first reflects what the district believes the house was worth on January 1. The second may match that figure or be lower when a cap applies.

HCAD's [2026 reappraisal information](https://hcad.org/hcad-resources/reappraisal/2026-reappraisal-values) describes its tax-appraisal role and the January 1 valuation point. That date matters when comparing a tax record with a proposal made later in the year.

Write down the tax year and the precise label next to the number. Avoid calling every figure on a notice the appraisal. These HCAD references concern Harris County; a property in Fort Bend County has a different appraisal district.

## Put each number beside the question it answers

<div style="overflow-x:auto" role="region" aria-label="Property value and offer comparison" tabindex="0">

| Number or document | Question to ask before comparing |
|---|---|
| HCAD market value | Which January 1 and which recorded property facts? |
| HCAD appraised value | Is a cap affecting the figure? |
| Agent's suggested listing price | What condition, preparation and comparable sales does it assume? |
| Written cash proposal | What current condition, responsibilities and terms does it reflect? |
| Estimated seller proceeds | Which costs and payoff amounts were deducted? |

</div>

A suggested listing price is not a completed sale. A buyer's proposed price is not your take-home amount. Keeping those distinctions visible makes the conversation more useful than comparing two large numbers without their assumptions.

## Check the house facts before debating a discount

Compare the record with what you know today: building size, additions, condition, occupancy and significant work still needed. Identify uncertainties instead of substituting your own unsupported estimate.

Ask a buyer to explain the practical issues affecting its proposal. If repairs are part of that explanation, ask what scope is being considered. A vague statement that the house needs work is less useful than knowing which systems or conditions require evaluation.

For example, imagine a seller comparing an offer on a house with a nonworking AC against an agent's plan that assumes the AC will be repaired first. That is a hypothetical comparison of different sale conditions, not evidence that either price is correct. Our [AC replacement decision guide](/blog/replace-air-conditioner-before-selling-houston-house/) helps organize that separate spending question.

## Compare proceeds using consistent assumptions

Use the same known mortgage payoff and other property obligations in each scenario. Then separate costs that belong to a particular route: preparation, agreed selling expenses, concessions and the time you expect to keep the property.

Do not automatically subtract every possible expense from a listing or assume every expense disappears in a cash sale. Use the actual written proposals and clearly label anything still estimated. Our [cash-offer versus listing comparison](/blog/cash-offer-vs-listing-house-houston/) explains the broader tradeoffs.

A listing can offer wider market exposure and may produce a higher sale price. A current-condition cash purchase may reduce preparation and coordination. Convenience has value, but it does not establish that the cash route produces more money.

## Keep appraisal questions separate from the offer decision

If the record appears inaccurate, investigate that issue through HCAD or an appropriate professional. This guide does not determine protest eligibility, deadlines, exemptions or a corrected value.

Likewise, the property's value is different from the amount of tax owed. If delinquent taxes are part of the situation, use the [Houston back-tax selling guide](/blog/sell-house-behind-on-taxes-houston/) for that separate discussion.

When requesting a Cash As-Is review, describe the actual condition and explain which county figure you are comparing. Ask what drives the proposal, compare alternatives and choose based on a clear sale plan—not an unexplained percentage of a tax number.
