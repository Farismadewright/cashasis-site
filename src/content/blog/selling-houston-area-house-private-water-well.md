**If your Houston-area house uses a private water well, organize the well's identity, water-test results and equipment history separately before selling.** A well log does not establish today's water quality, and a pump that operates does not answer every question a buyer may have about the supply.

This guide concerns a house with its own household well. First establish what actually serves the property; do not assume a rural-looking house uses a well or that every well arrangement has the same requirements.

## Confirm the supply you are describing

Identify whether the house receives water from a private well, a public system or another arrangement. Note any uncertainty about shared equipment, service rights or the source used by the house. Ask the relevant professionals to review those issues rather than inferring them from the yard or mailing address.

TCEQ's [drinking-water quality guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/drinkingwater/my-drinking-water-quality) distinguishes public-system information from private-well testing. For a private well, it places responsibility for regular testing on the owner and recommends an accredited drinking-water laboratory.

That is a reason to verify the actual evidence, not a statement that a particular test or certificate is universally required for every sale. Confirm the transaction's requirements with the buyer, closing team and appropriately qualified professionals.

## Gather three different kinds of evidence

<div style="overflow-x:auto" role="region" aria-label="Private well sale information organizer" tabindex="0">

| Record | What it can help explain |
|---|---|
| Well log or drilling report | Which well was constructed and the recorded construction details |
| Laboratory report | What the submitted sample was tested for and what was reported |
| Pump and equipment records | Work performed, dates, components and actual warranty terms |
| Treatment-system records | What equipment is installed and what maintenance is documented |
| Professional evaluation | Which questions were assessed and which remain unresolved |

</div>

Keep the full document rather than a cropped result or an undated summary. Write down who produced it and which well or system it concerns. Do not turn a limited finding into an assurance about everything on the property.

## Find an older well report without guessing at a match

TCEQ's [water-well information directory](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/drinkingwater/SWAP/wells.html) suggests gathering construction information and points to Texas Water Development Board data and the state's scanned well reports. It also notes that a groundwater district might hold a well log.

A nearby record is a lead for further checking, not proof it describes your supply. Compare available identifying construction details and ask for help if the match is uncertain. Keep a missing record labeled missing instead of borrowing the details of a neighboring well.

Do not publish a packet of personal account details or identification documents when marketing the house. Share necessary property records through the verified transaction contacts.

## Ask what a water result actually covers

Look for the sample date, sampling location, laboratory, analyses and complete findings. Ask the laboratory or qualified professional to explain the scope and any recommended next step. Follow their sampling instructions; this article does not provide a testing or treatment procedure.

Water appearance and a memory that it tasted fine are not substitutes for that review. An older report also should not be presented as a guarantee of current conditions. Be specific about what is known, what has changed and what has not been checked.

The same distinction applies to equipment. A pump invoice may establish that billed work occurred; it does not, by itself, certify drinking-water quality or settle every operating question.

## Keep the well separate from septic and condition disclosure

Water supply and wastewater disposal need separate records. If the property also has a septic system in Fort Bend County, use the [Fort Bend septic-sale checklist](/blog/selling-fort-bend-house-septic-system/) alongside this guide rather than treating one file as evidence for the other.

Known conditions and genuinely unknown information also belong in the wider sale conversation. Our [Texas seller-disclosure checklist](/blog/texas-as-is-sale-seller-disclosure-checklist/) helps organize that discussion; an as-is proposal does not replace applicable duties.

## Compare a sale before committing to equipment work

A listing may suit an owner seeking wider exposure who can coordinate the preparation and buyer evaluations. A Cash As-Is proposal may offer another route based on the property as it stands. Neither approach guarantees acceptance of a particular well, water result or closing timetable.
