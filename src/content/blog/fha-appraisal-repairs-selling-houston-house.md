**If a buyer’s FHA lender says your Houston house needs a repair, get the actual condition and the lender’s clearance requirements in writing before hiring anyone.** Then check who has agreed to pay and how much time remains. A required repair can affect the loan even if both sides agreed to sell as-is, but it does not automatically make the seller responsible for the bill.

## Find out what is holding up the loan

Ask the buyer or their agent to get the exact requirement from the lender. Is it a repair, a specialist’s report, or evidence that work is already complete? Who must verify it, and when does the lender need that proof? A buyer’s inspection request and a lender’s funding condition are separate. [HUD explains why an appraisal does not replace a buyer’s home inspection](https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/99-32att.pdf). Its [FHA Single Family Housing Policy Handbook](https://www.hud.gov/hud-partners/single-family-handbook-4000-1) addresses appraisal conditions and property requirements.

A condition on the house is also different from [a low appraisal that changes the financing numbers](/blog/low-lender-appraisal-selling-houston-house/). A lower price may change the loan math, but it does not repair a property defect or clear a lender condition.

## Read the contract before offering to pay

If you signed the [TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract (Resale)](https://www.trec.texas.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-forms/20-19_4.pdf), read Paragraph 7E and any amendments. In that form, neither side has to pay for lender-required repairs or treatments unless they agree otherwise in writing. The form also describes termination and return of earnest money when the parties cannot agree to pay. Your signed terms and facts matter, so get advice from your agent or a Texas real estate attorney before relying on a deadline, a termination right, or a deposit outcome.

If you decide to negotiate, write down the scope, who pays, the completion date, and what counts as proof. Verify whether the job requires permits or a licensed contractor, and check whether there is enough time for the lender’s review after work is complete. A credit on the closing statement does not by itself show that a required repair is done.

## Compare finishing the repair with selling as-is

For a small, well-defined repair, keeping a financed buyer may make sense if you can get a firm quote and the lender can review the work in time. For a larger issue with no firm scope or start date, compare that path with selling as-is. A cash buyer has no FHA lender condition when the purchase truly does not depend on that loan, but can still inspect the house, negotiate based on its condition, or offer less. First confirm whether you are free to accept another offer.

**Hypothetical:** A financed sale would leave you $92,000 before the newly required work. If the repair and verification are estimated at $3,500 and extra holding costs at $1,000, the revised estimate is $87,500. A separate as-is offer that would leave you $86,000 is $1,500 lower on those assumptions. The choice depends on whether that repair quote is firm and whether the buyer’s lender can clear the work by closing.
